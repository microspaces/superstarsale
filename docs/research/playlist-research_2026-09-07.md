## Video 1: Casey Builds Stuff — "Reviewing crappy rip offs of my successful app" *(analysis — business)*

- **Business model:** Founder of Dispersed (~$30/yr freemium wild-camping map app) reviews 6 vibe-coded clones head-to-head. His moat stack: proprietary data layer (phoned dozens of ranger districts to map where camping is actually legal — camping is legal on <10% of the roads in federal MVUM data), a UGC flywheel (10K active users submit hundreds of campsite locations monthly), community distribution (expo booths, workshops, build-in-public YouTube as top-of-funnel).
- **Revenue model:** **$24K ARR**; $7K/mo subscriptions at July peak, $5.5K August; ~60% of 125 active trials convert within 7 days. Competitor economics: Wildroute.ai charges $200/yr with an estimated $50K sunk; Grover.ai's AI-chat search costs ~$1.50 in tokens per query — unit-economics suicide for a map search; "Campsite" is free with no visible monetization (business-model mystery, possible data play).
- **Replication steps:** (1) pick a niche with fragmented public data + passionate users; (2) AI-build the app shell fast — the shell is not the product; (3) build the proprietary data layer competitors can't copy (his phone calls); (4) undercut the clone-tier price ($30 vs $120-200/yr); (5) seed UGC so the product improves itself; (6) distribute where the niche physically gathers; (7) watch trials as the leading indicator.
- **Automation potential:** 5/10 — vibe coding automates the shell; the moat (data acquisition calls, UGC moderation, community presence) is deliberately manual.
- **Quality:** 9/10 — strongest receipts in the batch: live RevenueCat dashboard figures, named competitors tested on camera. Self-reported but internally consistent.
- **Verdict: Worth Trying**
- **Key actions:** treat "AI built my app" as table stakes — moats are data + UGC + distribution; price below the clone tier; don't use AI-chat as spatial search UX (token COGS); monitor free competitors for hidden data plays.
- **Red flags:** heavy seasonality (self-acknowledged); all numbers self-reported; his own products get generous framing; free rival could compress pricing.

## Cross-Video Synthesis

**Three threads across ten videos.** (1) **Production cost has collapsed; moats haven't.** Batch A's throughline: vibe-coded clones ship fast and still lose to proprietary data (Casey's ranger-district phone calls), distribution, and verified proof. The GPT-6 Astra wave (3 titles) is the same story at the tool layer — every demonstration is procedural, every effectiveness claim is asserted, and the only verified AI-product profits in the batch belong to the creators selling the tutorials. (2) **Own the relationship, not the platform.** The Guerrilla Publisher (authors vs Amazon), the Creative Loafing buyout (locals vs extractive chains), and even Shortfundly's pitch all argue the same structure from different directions: whoever creates demand should own the customer record and the transaction. The employee-group buyout of distressed legacy media is a genuine **new variant of the acquisitions theme** worth a dedicated dig — small checks, brand equity, ad-book transferability. (3) **Verification techniques are becoming the content.** Wholesale Ted's YPP-membership check and Casey's live dashboard walkthrough are themselves reusable audit methods — use them on every future batch. Against the known-theme list: ~7 of 10 are re-skins (Etsy/POD/KDP, faceless channels, micro-SaaS, creator funnels, courses). New or sharpened: **AI UGC ad studios as a service**, **Amazon on-listing video commissions**, **synthetic fashion-model affiliate accounts**, **UEFN engagement-payout mechanics** (engaged-minute rates + season-timing), the **zero-cost AI thumbnail pipeline**, the **casino-comp-for-creator-traffic sponsorship model**, and the **local-media employee buyout**. Numbers-to-credibility inversion held all night: the vendor with the most dollar figures was least verifiable; the actual owner gave none.
