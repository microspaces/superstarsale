## 2) How I'd Start in Real Estate in 2026 (If I Had to Start Over) — Pace Morby (273,679 views)

**Business model:** Creative-finance real estate acquisition: seller financing ("the seller becomes the bank"), subject-to, and RV park deals. Entry path is deal-brokering — find motivated sellers, bring deals to established buyers for a finder's fee + equity, then graduate to owning deals yourself. Strong emphasis on acquisition strategy (lead flow, motivated sellers) vs. exit strategy (Airbnb, Section 8, flips).

**Revenue model:**
- Deal 1: ~$50,000 finder's fee + 10% ownership for brokering an RV park; 20–40% equity on deal 2.
- Case study: $90,000 check on a second park; bought a $5M park solo on the third deal.
- San Angelo 43-unit: $3M purchase, seller-financed at 3% interest, $16,000/mo payment amortized over 50 years; property grosses $40,000/mo, net ~$11,000/mo ($8–10k/mo target underwritten up front).
- RV park stays average 3–4 months (oil workers), not 7-day turns.

**Replication steps:**
1. Join the free sub-to underwriting Zooms (4/week); attend one weekly for ~3 months to learn underwriting.
2. Daily 20-minute scans of Craigslist and LoopNet for stale, overpriced listings (100+ days on market = motivation).
3. Call stale-listing sellers; diagnose pain with the terms script: "I can't pay cash, but I'll get near your number with monthly payments."
4. Reverse-engineer terms from your required net (e.g., "$10k/mo after debt service, utilities, capex") and offer full price for low payment/long amortization.
5. First deals: bring the underwritten deal to an active RV-park buyer for a finder's fee (~10% of deal value) plus a small equity slice.
6. Record a promissory note/lien with the state; scale equity share each deal until you buy solo.
7. Match the asset to the exit AFTER acquisition.

**Automation score:** 3/10 — income is real and semi-passive once owned, but acquisition is deeply manual: cold calls, negotiation, due diligence, asset management. Only lead scanning is automatable.

**Quality score:** 8/10 — passes the receipts test: named principals, named property (43 units, San Angelo, Big Spring TX), live video walkthrough at the actual park, verifiable payment structure.

**Verdict: Worth Trying** (for someone with time + risk tolerance; a skill-and-hustle path, not passive)

**Key actions:**
- Sit in one sub-to underwriting Zoom per week for 90 days (free education).
- Build a stale-listing pipeline: LoopNet/Craigslist >90–100 DOM in RV-park markets.
- Practice the terms script on 10 sellers before structuring anything.
- Target finder's-fee deals first — income without ownership risk while learning.

**Red flags:**
- Mentorship-funnel incentive: the free path leads into the paid sub-to ecosystem.
- 50-year seller-financed amortization is exotic; promissory notes carry default/due-on-sale risk not discussed.
- Headline numbers assume a below-market basis already negotiated; survivorship-biased.
- Seller-finance paperwork on income property is where beginners botch deals without an attorney.

**Revenue model (named examples):**

- Email marketing: $25,000/mo (Busy Budgeter).

- KDP: creator's own "few hundred $/mo" since 2012 (honestly disclosed).
- Drop servicing: $6,000/mo cleaning business, outsourced labor.
- Faceless YouTube: up to $14,000/mo ad revenue, AI tools + freelancers.

- Licensing: $300,000 royalties from a card game licensed to Mattel.
- Honeygain: up to ~$30/mo.
- Rentals: claimed $155,000/mo cash flow (almost certainly a transcription artifact / exaggeration vs. the creator's usual ~$15k claims).

**Replication steps:**
1. Pick a category matching capital/time: zero-capital (KDP, printables, POD, content) vs. capital (car wash, laundromat, storage, rentals).
2. For digital: create the asset, list on Etsy/Amazon/Udemy, let platform delivery automate fulfillment.
3. For content: publish consistently until ranking; layer affiliate/email automation sequences.
4. For faceless YouTube: build a freelancer/AI-tool pipeline for scripts, voiceover, editing.

**Verdict: Mixed** — a good idea-menu, not a playbook. Use it to shortlist 1–2 categories, then go deep elsewhere.

**Key actions:**
- Highest signal-to-effort for beginners: Etsy printables and KDP (zero cost, fully automated delivery).
- With capital: laundromat/car wash acquisition is the most proven "automated" local route here.
- Avoid Honeygain-class apps ($30/mo ceiling, privacy risk) — not a business.
- Verify any success-story figure before treating it as a benchmark.

- 19 ideas in 15 minutes = no depth on any; survivorship bias across all case studies.
- $155k/mo rental claim is not credible as stated.
- Old examples presented as current-2026 opportunities; Etsy/KDP/stock-photo markets are far more saturated now.
- CTA funnels viewers into a free email course.

## 4) How to Make Money on X (Twitter) in 2026: X Monetization Explained — Mr. Money (1,615 views)

**Business model:** X native creator monetization — four streams: (1) ad revenue sharing, (2) creator subscriptions, (3) tips, (4) affiliate + sponsorships. Positioning: use X as a discovery engine, monetize via off-platform affiliate/product income.

**Revenue model:**
- Ad share: ~$8–12 per 1M impressions; 5M impressions/3mo (minimum) ≈ $200–400/mo; 20M/mo ≈ $800–1,600; 100M/mo ≈ $4–8K.
- Median payout: ~$130/mo; most monetized creators under $200/mo.
- Top 1% (100K+ followers, finance/tech/politics): $5K+/mo; top 0.1% (~500 accounts globally): $25K+/mo.
- Subscriptions: 97% of gross until $50K lifetime, then 90%; example: 1,000 subs × $5 = $5,000/mo.
- Affiliate: 5K engaged niche followers ≈ $300–1,500/mo.

**Replication steps:**
1. Pick one specific niche (finance/tech/AI earn more per impression).
2. Buy X Premium ($8–16/mo), verify account.
3. Post consistently toward 500 followers + 5M impressions/3mo (realistically needs ~10K+ active followers).
4. Turn on tips immediately (no follower minimum).
5. At thresholds, enable ad revenue sharing; at 2K verified followers, subscriptions.
6. Simultaneously drop affiliate links in threads in your niche.
7. Negotiate brand sponsorships once you show niche authority (works from 3–10K followers).
8. Build off-platform assets (email list, own product) so income survives policy shifts.

**Automation score:** 2/10 — consistent human posting is the engine; niche authority can't be meaningfully automated without triggering spam/fake-follower revocation.

**Quality score:** 5/10 — requirements and rates are platform-documented and the video honestly leads with the median ($130/mo). But zero live dashboards, no named business of the creator's own.

**Verdict: Mixed** — real mechanics, honestly presented, but median economics make this a weak standalone play. Viable only as a distribution layer for offers you control.

**Key actions:**
- Treat X as top-of-funnel, not the business.
- Niche down (SaaS pricing > generic productivity) — fewer competitors, higher RPM.
- Enable tips day one; affiliate links before hitting impression thresholds.
- Prioritize subscriptions over ad share for predictable recurring revenue.

**Red flags:**
- Median payout $130/mo — expect the median, not the headline.
- X can modify/cancel/revoke monetization at its own discretion; thresholds shifted multiple times in 2026.
- Income concentrated in top 1%; survivorship-bias screenshots everywhere.
- Platform dependency — a suspension wipes income overnight.

## 5) How I Built a 7-Figure Productized Offer (and Escaped Agency Life) — Greg Hickman (1,100 views)

**Business model:** Productized consulting — collapse a custom agency into ONE offer, ONE package, ONE ideal client, ONE repeatable delivery system ("build the engine; let the client's team be the pit crew"). Clients' teams execute using your system. Secondary: sells a program teaching this (~900 students).

**Revenue model:** Productized offer hit a 7-figure run rate in ~10 months and became the top revenue source. ~900 program participants. No price points given.

**Replication steps:**
1. Audit your agency/service: does each new client make your calendar fuller? Are hires solving problems or creating management load?
2. Strip to one clear offer for one clear ideal client.
3. Package your repeatedly-delivered capability into one repeatable delivery system (the "engine").
4. Reprice/reposition: sell the system, not your labor — client's team executes.
5. Kill custom work and scope creep.
6. Shrink the team to what the single delivery system needs.
7. Let the productized line become the primary revenue stream; keep DFY only if it stays lean.

**Automation score:** 3/10 — leverage, not automation: one repeatable system, minimal founder time, client teams deliver. Still human-driven consulting.

**Quality score:** 4/10 — named, credible clients lend real credibility. But the 7-figure claim is entirely self-reported: no dashboards, no revenue proof. Explicitly a funnel to the paid program.

**Verdict: Worth Trying** (with caveats) — productized consulting is a proven model for service operators, and the "engine builder" framing is genuinely useful. Consume as a framework, not a course pitch.

**Key actions:**
- Identify the ONE capability you deliver repeatedly across clients.
- Turn it into a fixed-scope, fixed-price package with a written delivery system.
- Pitch "we build your team the engine" instead of done-for-you retainers.
- Track founder-hours per client before/after — that's the real metric.
- Ignore the program upsell; the free framework is 80% of the value.

**Red flags:**
- 7-figure claim unverifiable; no receipts.
- Entire video is a lead magnet for a ~900-student program.
- Zero tactical specifics — all narrative, no pricing or templates.

## Key Takeaways

1. **No new business models today.** All five videos map to known themes (real estate, vending/machines, digital products, creator monetization, productized consulting). No theme graduated to the candidate list.
2. **Receipts separate signal from noise.** The only video with named properties, named buyers, and checkable deal structures (Pace Morby) is the one worth a weekend; TAM-projection listicles (New Business Ideas) are pure filler.
3. **Fully automatable paths remain the same small set:** KDP, Etsy printables, POD, stock photos — zero capital, platform-automated delivery, but increasingly saturated markets (most cited examples were 2012–2019 era).
4. **Median X creator earns ~$130/mo.** Treat X as distribution, not income; subscriptions beat ad share, and off-platform assets are mandatory.
5. **"Sell the engine, not the pit crew"** is the day's best framework for anyone in agency work: one offer, one system, client's team executes — but it's leverage, not automation.
6. **Watch the funnels:** 4 of 5 videos funnel into paid programs, mentorship ecosystems, or course upsells. The free frameworks were consistently the most valuable part.
