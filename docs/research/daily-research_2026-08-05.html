Video 1: Niche Pursuits — "How This BORING Website Makes $35,000/Month!"↑ top

Business Model: Boring Utility Websites — Build free single-purpose tool websites (unit converters, typing tests, PDF tools) that rank on Google and monetize through display ads or freemium subscriptions. Case studies: unitconverter.net ($35K/mo), chartsheets.com ($40K/mo), typingtest.com ($44K+/mo), smallpdf.com ($10M+/yr).

Revenue Model: Display ads (RPM-based, $5+ per 1K impressions) or freemium subscriptions ($12/mo+). Traffic driven entirely by SEO for high-volume utility keywords.

Steps to Replicate:

1. Identify a boring/mundane task people search for (conversions, calculators, templates)

2. Build a simple free tool that solves it better than existing options

3. Optimize for SEO — target high-volume long-tail keywords

4. Monetize with display ads once traffic hits ~100K visitors/mo

5. Add premium features for subscription revenue once established

Automation Potential: 8/10 — Once built, these sites run themselves. Content is the tool itself, not ongoing posts. Traffic is organic/SEO. Ad revenue is passive. Only maintenance is keeping the site running and occasionally adding features.

Quality Score: 8/10 — Real revenue data with SimilarWeb verification. Multiple case studies with concrete numbers. No hidden upsell — the channel makes content about online business ideas. Honest about the work involved (SEO takes time).

Verdict: Strong — The "boring utility" thesis is one of the most defensible passive income models. High upfront SEO work, but once ranked, the tools generate income indefinitely with minimal maintenance. Best for someone willing to think small to earn big.

Key Action Items:

- Audit personal skills/knowledge for "boring" tool ideas (calculators, converters, templates)

- Research high-volume Google keywords with low competition in utility spaces

- Consider building a niche calculator or converter tool as an MVP

- RPM math: at $5 RPM, need 7M pageviews/mo for $35K — achievable for the right utility

2. Build a free monetization plan for them as an outreach gift (show value first)

5. Collect revenue share monthly — scale by partnering with more creators

But Gadzhi's presentation inflates ease and omits the difficulty of creator outreach, deal negotiation, and product quality. The framework for evaluating business models (unitary gain, startup capital, speed to first dollar, skill requirements, fulfillment complexity, scalability) is the most valuable takeaway.
