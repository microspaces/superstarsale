Key Takeaways

1. Directories are today's winner: passive income that's actually passive. Scrape a fragmented niche dataset, vibe-code the site once, template it across categories/cities, run display ads, and only sell $300/yr priority placements to businesses you can *prove* you're sending traffic ("permissionless marketing"). 80/20 outcomes are expected ΓÇö the portfolio, not any single site, is the asset.

2. "Build once, sell many" turns AI services into products. Koerner's gutter-company voice agent ($3,000 + $600/mo) gets cloned to every gutter company in the market. Pick verticals with many small identical businesses; the demo is the same app.

3. Newsletter economics are now fully specified: $1/sub acquisition via geo-radius Meta ads, $0.50/sub/mo from local sponsors, 2-month payback, template-to-next-city scaling. The only deliberately manual piece is the writing (it must feel local) ΓÇö curation/assembly can still be systematized.

4. Copy-don't-innovate is an automatable research workflow. Web Archive + SimilarWeb competitor timelines (pricing/copy/traffic overlays) can be scripted for any market you're evaluating ΓÇö and the "mirage opportunity" filter (obvious idea, zero successes = stay away) kills bad bets fast.

5. From the eBay cluster, keep only the mechanisms: consignment as zero-capital sourcing (50/50 splits), the 2-week draft bank as batched listing labor, free-postage-priced-in (70% of sales), and lot-multiplier assembly. The flipping model itself fails our automation bar.
