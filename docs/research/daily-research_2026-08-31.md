### 1. Chris Koerner on DOAC — Side-Hustle Portfolio Playbook

**Business model:** Not one model — a portfolio framework from an operator with 80+ launches (low hundreds of millions cumulative revenue). Today's actionable set: **AI implementation for SMBs**, **drop-servicing home services**, **niche directory websites**, **vending**, **wedding rentals**, **local email newsletters**, and **small RV park acquisition**. The meta-doctrine: copy proven models exactly (Web Archive + SimilarWeb reverse-engineering), avoid "mirage opportunities," and only start where you have an unfair advantage.

**How money is made (per sub-model):**
- **AI implementation for SMBs:** $500–$5,000 upfront build + ~20%/month maintenance. Example: after-hours AI voice agent for a gutter-cleaning company — $3,000 build + $500–600/mo. Key: build once per vertical, then copy-paste the same app to every competitor in the market.
- **Directory websites:** scrape a niche dataset (e.g., every dog park in Seattle), build with Replit/Lovable, monetize with Google display ads or $300/yr "priority placement" once you can prove you're sending a business traffic. Numbers game: some make $0, some make thousands/month. <$100 to start, zero employees.
- **Local email newsletters:** acquire subs at ~$1 each via geo-targeted Facebook ads, monetize at ~$0.50/subscriber/month via local sponsorships ($500/send at 1,000 subs) → **2-month payback**, then copy-paste the template to the next city.
- **Drop-servicing:** clean website + ads for a home service (garage repair, oil changes), subcontract fulfillment to a local operator, keep the margin on better UX/checkout (charge $80 for the $40 oil change).
- **Wedding rentals:** $300 built wedding arch rents for ~$1,000/wedding; distribute via wedding planners scraped from The Knot/Zola/WeddingWire.
- **RV parks:** buy 3–10 pad parks at a 10% cap rate with seller financing (~$5k + due diligence), force occupancy via free FB Marketplace listings + a vibe-coded website, revalue at 8% cap. His numbers: $3–4M/yr revenue, $400–600k net across ~35 parks.
- **Vending:** $500 machine + stock; location is the whole game (apartment lobbies win; his friend does 7 figures).

**Automation score: 7/10 (blended)** — Directories 9, local newsletters 7, AI implementation 6 (sales-manual, delivery copy-paste), drop-servicing 6, vending 4, wedding rentals 3, RV parks 3.

**Quality score: 8/10** — Highest-credibility guest in the set: verifiable exits (phone-screen reman business $2M→$9M/yr, the Buc-ee's merch store), specific unit economics throughout, no course pitch. Some stats are anecdotal (77% of SMB owners say AI is transformational, 5% use it meaningfully — directionally right, sourced loosely).

**Steps to replicate (directory portfolio — the most automatable):**
1. Pick 10 niche/local categories too fragmented for Google Maps (ice suppliers, dog parks, shooting ranges, ADA contractors)
2. Scrape/compile the dataset (scripted), build the site architecture once with Replit/Lovable
3. Copy-paste across categories/cities; add Google AdSense from day 1
4. Watch analytics for traffic winners → only then outreach: "I've been sending you customers — want priority placement for $300/yr?"
5. Kill zero-traffic sites at 6 months, redeploy the template

**Verdict: Worth Trying** (directories + newsletters + vertical-replicated AI agents) / Mixed (drop-servicing, vending, RV parks) — the RV park math is the biggest absolute money in today's set but is an operator business, not an automated one.

**Key action items:** Stand up the first 3 directory sites this week (template + scraped data + AdSense); run a $100 Replit/Lovable sprint to build one vertical AI voice-agent demo; note the "copy exactly, innovate later" and mirage-opportunity filters for every future deal review.

### 2. Greg Gottfried — 7 Passive Income Streams

**Business model:** A personal portfolio of passive digital-product streams: Amazon KDP (specifically **word-search puzzle books**), Amazon Merch on Demand (niche t-shirt designs), affiliate marketing via YouTube (video > blog articles), YouTube AdSense on **educational** content (higher CPM than entertainment), Etsy digital downloads (templates/spreadsheets), online courses (Udemy/Skillshare), plus HYSA interest.

**How money is made:**
- KDP: upload cover + interior PDF; Amazon prints, ships, and handles service forever. Claims income since 2018 including a 1+ year stretch without logging in. Word-search category called out as the best sub-niche because few sellers know how to generate the puzzles (lower competition)
- Merch on Demand: same print-on-demand mechanics, higher margin per unit than KDP; win via niche research into un-saturated design categories
- Affiliate: referral links inside product-review videos; his key data point — ~70% of internet traffic now prefers video over articles, so video reviews outperform blog reviews
- Digital downloads: one-time file creation (wedding invite templates, budgeting spreadsheets), marketplace handles delivery
- Courses: hardest to start (requires real expertise) but most passive per unit of knowledge

Claims $800k+ passive income last year across the portfolio (not shown on screen).

**Automation score: 8/10** — every stream is create-once/sell-forever with marketplace-fulfilled delivery. The creation phase itself is now highly automatable with AI (puzzle generation, design batching, template production), which the video doesn't even mention — that's upside.

**Quality score: 6/10** — real mechanics shown on screen, operator since 2018 (KDP/Merch), but heavy cross-promotion funnel to his own free tutorials, and no verifiable income proof. The word-search and "video beats blog" advice is genuinely useful.

**Steps to replicate:**
1. KDP: learn word-search puzzle generation (or script it), batch 5 themed books, upload with keyword-researched titles
2. Merch: niche research for low-competition design categories → batch simple text/graphic designs
3. Digital downloads: pick one template type you can make well (spreadsheets, invites), list on Etsy
4. Affiliate layer: film product-review videos with referral links rather than writing blog posts
5. Reinvest into whichever stream shows organic traction; courses only after real expertise exists

**Verdict: Mixed** — the model is proven and maximally passive, but KDP/Merch/POD are well-trodden (covered repeatedly in past reports) and AI has lowered the word-search moat he cites. Best treated as a low-cost portfolio slice, not a primary business.

**Key action items:** If entering, compete on **volume of niches** (automated production pipeline), not on any single listing; prioritize digital downloads + educational YouTube as the higher-leverage pair.

### 3. The Aussie Flipper — 23 eBay Lessons From 5 Years

**Business model:** Full-time eBay flipping (thrift/flea sourcing → listed resale) plus **consignment** (sell others' expensive items for a 50/50 profit split, 70/30 for one-offs — zero-cost inventory).

**How money is made:** Buy at 1/3 of sale price (the "triple your money" rule → ~2x after fees/postage), quality-over-quantity sourcing, 0.98% return rate maintained with returns ON (buyer confidence > return cost), 70% of eBay sales come from free-postage listings (price shipping into the item), keep a 2-week draft bank so listings go live daily without daily work.

**Automation score: 3/10** — sourcing, photographing, listing, and shipping are inherently manual. Systemizable pieces: the 48-hour list-it-or-stop-sourcing rule, the 2-week draft bank (batched listing), locked search filters for comp research, and consignment (removes capital requirement but adds relationship management).

**Quality score: 7/10** — 5-year full-time operator, platform-sourced data points (eBay's own 70%-free-postage and title-team stats), real mentoring-client examples, transparent about his own software/tool promos.

**Steps to replicate:** Enable Seller Hub; lock comp-research filters (sold + condition + country); adopt 48-hour rule + draft bank; offer free postage priced-in and returns on; take consignment for high-ticket items you can't afford to buy; ask vendors for their price first (his $50 offer on a $10-tagged $200 Batman figure).

**Verdict: Skip** for an automation-focused portfolio — lowest automation ceiling of today's set — but the consignment mechanism and draft-bank batching are worth stealing if you ever run a flipping sideline.

**Key action items:** Steal consignment (zero-COGS sourcing) and the 2-week draft bank (batched listing labor) as transferable tactics.

### 4. FlippyMcFlipperson — Thrift Flipping 101

**Business model:** Beginner thrift-store flipping on eBay: research brands before sourcing (YouTube/Instagram resellers, start with your own hobby knowledge), comp everything with the eBay app's sold filter, buy only on sell-through rate + margin.

**How money is made:** Spread-based arbitrage (example: $5 Honeywell thermostat → sold $58). Core formula taught: if 18 listed / 27 sold in 90 days, sell-through is healthy — buy; if 100 listed / 2 sold — leave it. Price at the average of the last 5–6 sold comps. SKU-binned inventory management.

**Automation score: 2/10** — physical sourcing and per-item listing/shipping dominate. The comp-check workflow (barcode scan → sold filter → STR math) is the only semi-scriptable step.

**Quality score: 6/10** — honest, competent beginner guide with a worked real example, but zero novel tactics beyond standard reseller canon.

**Steps to replicate:** Map a thrift route (Google Maps + MapQuest optimizer), scan-with-eBay-app everything, apply STR + margin thresholds, list with blank-background photos and full item specifics.

**Verdict: Skip** — well-executed but fully manual and thoroughly covered territory.

**Key action items:** None beyond the sell-through-rate heuristic, which generalizes to any marketplace sourcing decision.

### 5. Flippity Flip — Resale Business From Scratch

**Business model:** Start-to-first-sale eBay store build: seed inventory from your own house, then source flea markets + Goodwill weekly; everything bought on-channel gets listed in a public store (radical transparency — sold prices visible).

**How money is made:** Low-cost flips with full P&L shown (first sale: $1 military catalog → $18, ~$8 net after $5 ship + $3 fees). Embedded heuristics with real values: sealed hardcover box sets are always $15+, Survivor DVD seasons are rare and valuable in lots, list "want-to-keep" items high, batch same-series items into lots for multiplier pricing.

**Automation score: 2/10** — same physical-model constraints as #3/#4; slightly lower systemization (no draft bank, no consignment layer).

**Quality score: 6/10** — genuinely transparent (public store, real numbers, honest misses like the Q-tips and liquid nails), but beginner-diary format with limited strategic depth.

**Steps to replicate:** Declutter-seed a store (250 free listings), source flea/Goodwill with the app, list via "sell one like this" against sold comps, free shipping on lightweight items, ship with reused mailers + thermal printer.

**Verdict: Skip** for this portfolio — manual model, no new system.

**Key action items:** The "lot multiplier" idea (assemble incomplete series across trips, sell as sets) is the one transferable tactic.

## Key Takeaways

1. **Directories are today's winner: passive income that's actually passive.** Scrape a fragmented niche dataset, vibe-code the site once, template it across categories/cities, run display ads, and only sell $300/yr priority placements to businesses you can *prove* you're sending traffic ("permissionless marketing"). 80/20 outcomes are expected — the portfolio, not any single site, is the asset.
2. **"Build once, sell many" turns AI services into products.** Koerner's gutter-company voice agent ($3,000 + $600/mo) gets cloned to every gutter company in the market. Pick verticals with many small identical businesses; the demo is the same app.
3. **Newsletter economics are now fully specified:** $1/sub acquisition via geo-radius Meta ads, $0.50/sub/mo from local sponsors, 2-month payback, template-to-next-city scaling. The only deliberately manual piece is the writing (it must feel local) — curation/assembly can still be systematized.
4. **Copy-don't-innovate is an automatable research workflow.** Web Archive + SimilarWeb competitor timelines (pricing/copy/traffic overlays) can be scripted for any market you're evaluating — and the "mirage opportunity" filter (obvious idea, zero successes = stay away) kills bad bets fast.
5. **From the eBay cluster, keep only the mechanisms:** consignment as zero-capital sourcing (50/50 splits), the 2-week draft bank as batched listing labor, free-postage-priced-in (70% of sales), and lot-multiplier assembly. The flipping model itself fails our automation bar.
6. **No new discovery themes graduated today.** The wildcard query surfaced the passive-income/reselling cluster, all of which map to known categories (AI tools, local services, lead gen, newsletters, flipping, marketplaces, real estate). The theme pool is unchanged.
