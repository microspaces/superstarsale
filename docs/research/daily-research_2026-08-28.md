## Ranked Strategies

### 🥇 1. White-label HighLevel for one local-service vertical (Chris Koerner)

**Business model:** Resell GoHighLevel as "your" CRM/booking/AI phone system to one boring local niche (bounce houses, med spas, pest control, dentists, roofers). HighLevel publishes a public SaaSpreneur awards page with customer-count tiers (Gold 100+, Platinum 500+, Diamond 1,000+). Combine that with the vendor's public pricing and you can reverse-engineer revenue on a napkin. Koerner walks a roofing clone live: missed-call text-back + calendar booking.

**Revenue model:** HighLevel requires resale at ≥ $97/mo. Typical agencies charge $200–$497/mo. Software COGS is a flat ~$497/mo no matter if you have 10 or 10,000 customers. Claimed examples in the $50k–$200k/mo range after VA payroll.

**Steps to replicate:**
1. Pick one local vertical with missed-call pain (roofing, HVAC, med spa).
2. White-label HighLevel; ship one killer workflow first (missed-call SMS → booking link).
3. Price $197–$497/mo. Do not compete on $97.
4. Clone winners off the public awards page instead of inventing a niche.
5. Staff VAs for onboarding once you have 20+ accounts.

**Automation potential: 8/10** — Product is already built. Onboarding + support are the remaining human work; those are VA-able. Recurring SaaS, not hours-for-dollars.

**Quality score: 7/10** — Public awards page is a real, inspectable dataset. Live build is concrete. Affiliate disclosure is honest. Revenue figures are napkin math on customer-count floors, not audited P&Ls.

**Verdict: Worth Trying** — Best "clone a real business" map in the playlist. Durable because the buyer is a local operator who already pays for leads and hates missed calls.

**Key action items:**
- Screenshot the HighLevel awards page; list Gold winners in one vertical.
- Build a roofing/HVAC missed-call text-back demo in one afternoon.
- Sell the demo, not "AI."

### 🥈 2. Local AI implementation retainers — sell outcomes, not automations (Nick Ponte + Koerner/Ryan)

**Business model:** SMBs already believe they "need AI." They will not buy a Zapier flowchart. They will buy more leads, follow-up, content, and booked jobs. Ponte: $300–$2,000/mo retainers; claims $123k in one month from *local* SMB work (QuickBooks on-screen, separate from his coaching biz). Koerner's guest Ryan (24, introvert, Cincinnati): $15k/mo profit, $1k–$20k projects, $20 to start. Framework: DASA. Acquisition: friends/family/church/masterminds first, not cold LinkedIn slop.

Ponte's wedge: find local businesses with a Facebook page that used to post and went dark 6–24 months ago. Email subject: "are you still open?" Attach two weeks of free content already made for them.

**Revenue model:** Setup fee + monthly retainer. Five clients at $2,000/mo = $10k. Ryan's range is project-based $1k–$20k.

**Steps to replicate:**
1. Pull 50 local businesses with stale social + real phone numbers.
2. Generate 2 weeks of content for 10 of them (Claude + Canva/GHL).
3. Send the "still open?" email with the content attached. No pitch in email one.
4. Convert to $500–$2,000/mo "we post + we follow up leads."
5. Productize the follow-up layer (this is the actual AI, hidden).

**Automation potential: 7/10** — Content + follow-up sequences are automatable. Sales is still human, but the foot-in-door offer removes most of the selling. Once retained, fulfillment is a pipeline.

**Quality score: 6/10** — Ponte's $123k claim is a screenshot, not a cohort. Ryan's $15k/mo is a single anecdote. The *offer design* is the durable part: value in advance to a starving crowd that already paid for leads.

**Verdict: Worth Trying** — Highest overlap with SuperStarSale's local-business thesis. Do not sell "AI automation." Sell booked appointments.

**Key action items:**
- Build a 50-lead stale-page list in one metro.
- Ship the 2-week content gift to 10 of them this week.
- Productize missed-call follow-up as the paid upsell.

### 🥉 3. Agent-run local newsletter / directory (Greg Isenberg + Billy Howell / Grokbot)

**Business model:** One-person media company run by a tiny agent team. Billy Howell's *Arlington Bagel* newsletter: 6,000 local subs, weekly. Agents research local events/restaurants, fill Notion cards, write blurbs (Make.com once the prompt is stable), send via Beehiiv. Grokbot's constraint (limited agent slots, DM-style threads) is the feature — it forces one mission per bot instead of 40 abandoned ChatGPT threads.

**Revenue model:** Sponsorships, local ads, later a directory or events layer. Isenberg frames $2k–$25k/mo as the realistic band for these "small ideas."

**Steps to replicate:**
1. Pick a city + beat (food, kids events, board games, high-school sports).
2. Week 1: 3–6 named agents, one job each. No extra tools.
3. Week 2: execute. No tinkering.
4. Week 3: fire agents that stall, hire for the bottleneck.
5. Week 4: freeze the working workflow into Make/n8n so the agent is not re-deciding every week.

**Automation potential: 8/10** — Research + draft + layout are agent-native. Human job is editorial taste + selling the first three sponsors.

**Quality score: 7/10** — Working production system, not a hypothetical. Honest that Grokbot YouTube content is mostly wrong. Still a tool demo on someone else's podcast; 6k subs ≠ $25k proven.

**Verdict: Worth Trying** — Best "one-person company with agents" example in the playlist. Local beat > generic AI newsletter.

**Key action items:**
- One city, one beat, one send-day.
- Freeze blurbs into a deterministic automation once the voice is good.
- Sell 3 local sponsors before adding a second city.

### 4. Multilingual cloned-voice voicemails for realtors (Chris Koerner)

**Business model:** 40%+ of US homebuyers speak a language other than English at home (higher in Dallas/NYC/LA/Miami). Realtors cannot leave voicemails in those languages in their own voice. Clone 10–60 seconds of the agent's voice (Fish Audio S2 Pro, 80+ languages), generate a library: price-drop, new listing, showing confirm, missed appointment, home-anniversary. Charge $200–$500/mo.

**Revenue model:** Setup + monthly library refresh. Five realtor offices × $400 = $2k. Expand to insurance, immigration attorneys, audiobooks with the same pipeline.

**Automation potential: 7/10** — Generation is cheap (~$0.03/clip in the demo). Delivery + CRM integration still needs a wrapper. Sales motion is in-person lunch-and-learns at brokerages ("give away the secret, sell the implementation").

**Quality score: 6/10** — Live product demo is real. No paying-customer receipts. Voice-cloning consent/compliance is hand-waved. Accent quality in the Spanish sample was admittedly non-native — which Koerner likes, but buyers may not.

**Verdict: Mixed / Worth a test** — Narrow, starving-crowd offer. Treat as a wedge into realtor offices, then upsell the HighLevel stack from #1.

**Key action items:**
- Clone one willing agent's voice; produce 10 messages × 3 languages before the pitch.
- Lunch-and-learn at 3 brokerages. Leave a one-pager.

## Cross-playlist patterns

1. **The actual product is follow-up, not "AI."** Lead plumber (Shane #1), realtor voicemails (Koerner #2), Ponte's stale-page content, HighLevel missed-call text-back — same job: speed-to-lead for businesses already paying for demand.
2. **White-label > vibe-code.** HighLevel awards page vs "ask Claude to make me money" (which Shane demonstrates returns dropshipping/POD/faceless — garbage-in).
3. **Local beats global slop.** Arlington newsletter, Cincinnati AI implementer, Dallas realtor offices, roofing CRMs. Geography is the moat.
4. **Guru clustering is a quality tax.** Six Shane videos, three Wholesale Ted, four Koerner. Unique strategies ≈ 8, not 17.
5. **YouTube AdSense on synthetic media is the trap of this playlist.** Shane warns about the wipe; Ted then teaches you to do it anyway.
