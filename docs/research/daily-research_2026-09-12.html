1. My First 6 Months on Etsy Selling Digital Products — Sales, Revenue, & Profit Breakdown↑ top

Channel/Views/URL: Erika | 63,024 views | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Jy2b5KMIEGY

- Business model: Sell editable printable party stationery (invites, banners, signs, party decor) on Etsy. Designs made in Canva; the customer personalizes the template themselves and prints at home or at a print shop. Zero inventory, zero fulfillment, near-zero marginal cost.

- Revenue model: Month-by-month revenue shown live on the Etsy dashboard: Jan $0 (99 visits, 0 orders) → Feb $37.84 (5 orders) → Mar $266.21 (38 orders) → Apr ~$450 (47 orders) → May $1,175.98 (131 orders) → Jun $2,473–2,484 (368 orders). Profit after Etsy fees + ads: Mar $120.50, Apr $230, May $601, Jun $1,772. Roughly 50–72% of revenue converts to profit. Built on 360+ listings at 2–3 hrs/day.

- Replication steps:

1. Pick a printable-digital niche (party stationery here) with steady demand; design templates in Canva.

2. Deliver as an editable Canva template link so the customer self-personalizes — no per-order work.

3. List relentlessly: ~30 listings in month one, then 1–5 new listings/day; volume drives discovery.

4. Expect zero sales for ~1 month; treat months 1–3 as social-proof building, commit to 3 full months before judging.

5. Once organic sales appear, add Etsy ads (early $1/day tests were judged wasteful; scale only after listings convert).

6. Keep listing daily; 360+ listings producing ~10 orders/day at month six.

- Automation potential: 7/10 — template generation, SEO listing copy, mockups, and scheduling are scriptable (Canva API / templated workflows); customer personalization and support are self-serve. Human time is mostly design selection and QA.

- Quality/receipts: 9/10 — live screen-recorded Etsy dashboard with month-by-month visits/conversion/revenue/profit, including the $0 month. Realistic middle-ground numbers, named time investment. Caveats: listing counts estimated; the video doubles as channel growth content.

- Verdict: Worth Trying

- Key actions: Niche down to one printable-digital category; ship 30 listings before evaluating; use self-serve Canva template delivery; daily listing cadence; scale ads only after organic conversion exists. Benchmark: ~$1.7K profit/mo by month six at 2–3 hrs/day.

- Red flags: Saturated niche — success hinged on daily grind and 360+ listings; first-month $0 is the norm; income is real but modest (~$21K/yr pace at month 6); screenshots are her own shop only, no third-party verification; creator incentive to present the journey favorably.

2. Why Print on Demand Is Killing Your Gym's Apparel↑ top

Channel/Views/URL: Matt Albrizio | 2,514 views | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pWr9pVjN3Yo

- Business model: B2B service for gyms: bulk pre-order apparel campaigns (design, samples, marketing, fulfillment handled by vendor Forever Fierce) pitched as a higher-converting alternative to always-on POD web stores. The video itself is content-marketing/lead-gen for that service.

- Revenue model: Case anecdote: $15 profit/item; a gym went from 3–5 pre-order campaigns/yr × 35–50 items to 3–5 POD orders/month, losing $1,500–$2,500/yr profit. Speaker claims 25,000 closed t-shirt orders, 1–1.4M shirts sold, ~$25M lifetime t-shirt sales. No dashboards shown ("numbers on request" only).

- Replication steps:

1. Target niche communities with identity attachment (gyms, especially tourist-town gyms where drop-in merch sells).

2. Run time-boxed pre-order campaigns (3–5/yr) instead of an always-open POD store: limited designs, front-desk samples, explicit deadline.

3. Order in bulk (12–24 units) for quality and margin; avoid one-off POD prints.

4. Engineer buying triggers: physical samples where the audience gathers, countdown deadline, holiday-themed drops.

5. Amplify with community social proof: group-workout photo of buyers wearing shirts → visible unboxing/FOMO seeds the next campaign.

6. Keep SKU count deliberately tiny.

- Automation potential: 4/10 — campaign assets (designs, mockups, email/SMS blasts, order pages) are automatable, but the engine is relationship sales to gym owners plus physical bulk fulfillment. Higher as a playbook to copy for our own niche communities than as pitched.

- Quality/receipts: 3/10 — single unnamed gym anecdote ("$2,000 lost"), big unverifiable personal claims ($25M), no live dashboards; explicit pitch to book a call.

- Verdict: Skip (as a standalone business to copy — it's a vendor lead-gen piece; the pre-order mechanic itself is worth borrowing)

- Key actions: If ever selling apparel to a committed community, use deadline-driven pre-order drops with bulk printing, not always-on POD; put physical samples where the audience gathers; reuse the group-photo social-proof loop.

- Red flags: Pure sales funnel — "I'm not sharing this to sell you something" followed by a booking link; unverifiable $25M claims; no named client, no shown data.
