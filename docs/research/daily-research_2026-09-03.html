2. Website Flipping on Empire Flippers / Flippa — A1 Republic

- Business model: Buy under-monetized websites on marketplaces (Flippa for sub-$2,500 starter deals; Empire Flippers for verified larger assets), improve traffic/monetization/conversion, resell at a higher multiple.

- Revenue model: Capital gains on resale. Valuation rule of thumb: sites trade at 5–20x monthly profit. Example math: buy a $500 site earning $50/mo, grow it to $150/mo, resell for $750–$1,500.

- Steps to replicate:

1. Spend a week studying Flippa listings (categories: content, apps, ecommerce) comparing price vs. monthly *profit* (not revenue).

2. Buy only if you can answer "can I specifically improve this?" — check traffic source durability (organic > social > paid) and monetization gaps.

3. Improve one lever at a time: add affiliate links to ad-only blogs, fix design/speed/CTAs, add paid tiers to newsletters.

4. Relist and repeat; one deal, one site, one improvement at a time.

- Automation potential: 6/10 — Deal-finding can be alert/RSS automated and AI can assist content/monetization upgrades, but due diligence and improvement work are hands-on.

- Quality: 6/10 — Honest framing (explicitly "not passive income," no course), solid valuation math. Deductions: low-view channel, generic improvement advice, entry-level Flippa inventory is adverse-selection heavy.

- Verdict: ⚠️ Mixed — Real model but known territory (acquisitions theme covered repeatedly). Only compelling with genuine SEO/monetization skill and capital you can afford to lock up.

4. $500 AI Risk Snapshot Consulting — Cyber Women Society

- Business model: Productized consulting: a structured 1-hour "AI risk snapshot" assessment for small/medium businesses drowning in unsanctioned AI tool use ("shadow AI"), delivered as a 1–2 page report within 24–48 hours.

- Revenue model: $500 flat per snapshot; presenter reports most snapshots convert into ~$5,000 remediation/policy engagements within 30 days ($5,500 per client claimed).

- Steps to replicate (the 5-area framework):

1. Tool inventory: "Does your team use any AI writing, summarizing, coding, or support tools — even free ones?" Document sanctioned vs. shadow AI.

2. Data exposure: "Walk me through a typical task — what are employees pasting in?" (customer data, PII, financials, contracts).

3. Vendor risk: Check each tool's data-use policy — retention, training use, enterprise-tier protections (e.g., free ChatGPT retains/trains on inputs; API/enterprise does not).

4. Access & auth: Shared logins? Personal accounts on company devices? MFA? Offboarding?

5. Policy gap: Ask for their AI acceptable-use policy — if they can't produce it in 30 seconds, it doesn't exist (instant upsell: write one).

6. Deliver: executive summary + low/med/high risk matrix + top 3 findings + specific fixes. Upsell remediation.

- Automation potential: 7/10 — Intake questionnaire, report generation, risk-matrix templating, and outreach are all automatable; the 1-hour conversation and trust-based close stay human. Strong productization = highly repeatable.

- Quality: 7/10 — Complete, specific framework with exact questions and pricing; real conversion numbers; honest that it's a foot-in-the-door offer. Deductions: solo practitioner claims, community funnel at the end, low view count (unproven at scale).

- Verdict: ✅ Worth Trying — Today's most novel *actionable* model: it productizes a compliance fear that didn't exist 18 months ago. Validated from two directions today (this + BT's SMB-education push). This is exactly what the graduated "AI cybersecurity for small businesses" wildcard theme predicted.
