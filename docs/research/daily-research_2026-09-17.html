2) How I'd Start in Real Estate in 2026 (If I Had to Start Over) — Pace Morby (273,679 views)↑ top

Business model: Creative-finance real estate acquisition: seller financing ("the seller becomes the bank"), subject-to, and RV park deals. Entry path is deal-brokering — find motivated sellers, bring deals to established buyers for a finder's fee + equity, then graduate to owning deals yourself. Strong emphasis on acquisition strategy (lead flow, motivated sellers) vs. exit strategy (Airbnb, Section 8, flips).

Revenue model:

- Deal 1: ~$50,000 finder's fee + 10% ownership for brokering an RV park; 20–40% equity on deal 2.

- Case study: $90,000 check on a second park; bought a $5M park solo on the third deal.

- San Angelo 43-unit: $3M purchase, seller-financed at 3% interest, $16,000/mo payment amortized over 50 years; property grosses $40,000/mo, net ~$11,000/mo ($8–10k/mo target underwritten up front).

- RV park stays average 3–4 months (oil workers), not 7-day turns.

Replication steps:

1. Join the free sub-to underwriting Zooms (4/week); attend one weekly for ~3 months to learn underwriting.

2. Daily 20-minute scans of Craigslist and LoopNet for stale, overpriced listings (100+ days on market = motivation).

3. Call stale-listing sellers; diagnose pain with the terms script: "I can't pay cash, but I'll get near your number with monthly payments."

4. Reverse-engineer terms from your required net (e.g., "$10k/mo after debt service, utilities, capex") and offer full price for low payment/long amortization.

5. First deals: bring the underwritten deal to an active RV-park buyer for a finder's fee (~10% of deal value) plus a small equity slice.

6. Record a promissory note/lien with the state; scale equity share each deal until you buy solo.

7. Match the asset to the exit AFTER acquisition.

Automation score: 3/10 — income is real and semi-passive once owned, but acquisition is deeply manual: cold calls, negotiation, due diligence, asset management. Only lead scanning is automatable.

Quality score: 8/10 — passes the receipts test: named principals, named property (43 units, San Angelo, Big Spring TX), live video walkthrough at the actual park, verifiable payment structure.

Verdict: Worth Trying (for someone with time + risk tolerance; a skill-and-hustle path, not passive)

Key actions:

- Sit in one sub-to underwriting Zoom per week for 90 days (free education).

- Build a stale-listing pipeline: LoopNet/Craigslist >90–100 DOM in RV-park markets.

- Practice the terms script on 10 sellers before structuring anything.

- Target finder's-fee deals first — income without ownership risk while learning.

Red flags:

- Mentorship-funnel incentive: the free path leads into the paid sub-to ecosystem.

- 50-year seller-financed amortization is exotic; promissory notes carry default/due-on-sale risk not discussed.

- Headline numbers assume a below-market basis already negotiated; survivorship-biased.

- Seller-finance paperwork on income property is where beginners botch deals without an attorney.

5) How I Built a 7-Figure Productized Offer (and Escaped Agency Life) — Greg Hickman (1,100 views)↑ top

Business model: Productized consulting — collapse a custom agency into ONE offer, ONE package, ONE ideal client, ONE repeatable delivery system ("build the engine; let the client's team be the pit crew"). Clients' teams execute using your system. Secondary: sells a program teaching this (~900 students).

Revenue model: Productized offer hit a 7-figure run rate in ~10 months and became the top revenue source. ~900 program participants. No price points given.

Replication steps:

1. Audit your agency/service: does each new client make your calendar fuller? Are hires solving problems or creating management load?

2. Strip to one clear offer for one clear ideal client.

3. Package your repeatedly-delivered capability into one repeatable delivery system (the "engine").

4. Reprice/reposition: sell the system, not your labor — client's team executes.

5. Kill custom work and scope creep.

6. Shrink the team to what the single delivery system needs.

7. Let the productized line become the primary revenue stream; keep DFY only if it stays lean.

Automation score: 3/10 — leverage, not automation: one repeatable system, minimal founder time, client teams deliver. Still human-driven consulting.

Quality score: 4/10 — named, credible clients lend real credibility. But the 7-figure claim is entirely self-reported: no dashboards, no revenue proof. Explicitly a funnel to the paid program.

Verdict: Worth Trying (with caveats) — productized consulting is a proven model for service operators, and the "engine builder" framing is genuinely useful. Consume as a framework, not a course pitch.

Key actions:

- Identify the ONE capability you deliver repeatedly across clients.

- Turn it into a fixed-scope, fixed-price package with a written delivery system.

- Pitch "we build your team the engine" instead of done-for-you retainers.

- Track founder-hours per client before/after — that's the real metric.

- Ignore the program upsell; the free framework is 80% of the value.

Red flags:

- 7-figure claim unverifiable; no receipts.

- Entire video is a lead magnet for a ~900-student program.

- Zero tactical specifics — all narrative, no pricing or templates.
