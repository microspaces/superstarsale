1) I Tried The LAZIEST Way to Make Money With AI#

Channel: Mark Tilbury | 4,470,599 views | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LlhTEttKcwQ

Business model#

AI (virtual) influencer accounts: synthetic consistent characters posted to Instagram, monetized via digital products. The video surveys the full monetization menu found in the wild — brand deals (Lil Miquela ~$11M/yr, Prada/Calvin Klein), product promotion, affiliate (TikTok Shop), ad revenue (unavailable for IG Reels), physical and digital products — then executes digital products only. Pipeline: Claude (character + scripts via Claude skills) → Higsfield (images, voice, video, Cinema Studio) → Instagram → Stan Store (workbook).

Revenue model#

Documented 7-day run: 23 sales × $12 = $276 revenue; costs $99 (Higsfield Ultra) + $19.52 PayPal fees = $118.52; profit $157.48. Claude free tier, IG Edits free, Stan Store 14-day trial = $0. Quoted but unverifiable third-party claims: "$100K in last 30 days", "$200K profit", "$13K", Lil Miquela "$11M/yr".

Replication steps#

1. Spy on competing AI influencers; catalog niches and every monetization method they use.

2. Pick a niche from health / wealth / relationships (he found wealth underexploited by AI accounts).

3. In Claude (free), generate character profiles — name, backstory, distinct look, USP. He built 3 characters, one per niche.

4. In Higsfield (connected to Claude as a custom connector), generate consistent full-body + close-up character sheets on white background, realistic skin; iterate age/details; generate 4–5 consistent location scenes per character.

5. Install free Claude skills (content engine, video-prompt, digital product) to generate content ideas, scripts, and video prompts without manual writing.

6. Generate a voice: upload character image + sample line to Higsfield audio with a Claude-written voice description prompt.

7. In Higsfield Cinema Studio: 9:16, 1080p, reference the character image for consistency; generate talking clips + B-roll; add captions in a free editor.

8. Launch Instagram accounts with AI-generated profile pics and explicit AI disclosure in the bio ("your AI grandpa").

9. Post 10 videos per account across all 3 characters; crown the engagement winner and double down.

10. Monetize the winner: generate a structured, interactive digital product (30-day habit workbook) via the Claude digital-product skill; human-review it; sell on Stan Store ($15.99 list, $12 discount), link in bio.

11. Scale: cross-post the same assets to TikTok and YouTube Shorts; layer affiliate (TikTok Shop), brand deals, physical products.

Automation potential: 9/10#

Character generation, scripts, voice, video, and the product itself are all AI-generated in one Claude↔Higsfield loop. Remaining human work: account setup, safety review, posting, analytics judgment.

Quality: 7/10#

Receipts test: PARTIAL PASS. Real receipts on his own run — Stan Store sales count, PayPal fee line, named costs, and an honest failed prediction (he doubled down on Amos; abandoned Vivian then went viral at 250K views). 2) How To Make BUCKETS of Money In SERVICE Businesses#

Channel: Alex Hormozi | 698,345 views | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=m-7VjocC76M

Business model#

Offer-architecture framework, not a niche: six offer types — physical product, digital product, physical service, digital service, physical access (experiences), digital access (events, direct access to a person). Value comes from PAIRING categories to escape commodity pricing. Core play: a high-ticket access tier — a more individualized, higher-touch version of your existing solution sold to a small cohort (e.g., 5 people, 1:1 or small group), 12-month outcome guarantee ("or I keep working with you for free"), fully prepaid, no discount — for a fast cash injection.

Revenue model#

Portfolio claim: acquisition.com ~$85M/yr (self-reported; categories named, business names withheld). Offer sizing: "extra $5K / $10K / $20K / $50K" depending on client-base size. Digital products/access ≈ "almost 100% margins." No per-unit pricing or cohort revenue example → no concrete numbers for the viewer.

Replication steps#

1. Classify your current offer on the 6-type grid (product/service × physical/digital/access).

2. Pair in an adjacent category to add differentiated value (product + digital product; service + software; anything + access).

3. Weight the mix toward digital products and digital access — near-100% margin, scales without headcount, raises enterprise value.

4. If cash-strapped: design the access offer — 5 slots only, small group or 1:1, one big outcome, 12-month window, guarantee = work free until achieved, full period prepaid.

5. Charge premium with zero discount — the value is access + expertise, not price.

6. Deliberately undersell: if you could sell 20, sell 5; if 100, sell 20. Keep ~80% of demand pent-up so future launches still sell out.

7. Grow the audience base so each subsequent cohort's slot count grows (5→10→20…) — "controlled burn" of goodwill instead of liquidating it.

Automation potential: 2/10#

Pure strategy/sales craft; fulfillment is deliberately human high-touch. Only surrounding ops (scheduling, onboarding admin, content) could be automated.

Quality: 4/10#

Receipts test: FAIL. No dashboards, no named businesses (explicitly withheld), no cohort case numbers; $85M and "100% margin" figures are self-reported. Framework is internally coherent and consistent with his public track record, but this video presents zero verifiable receipts.

Verdict: Worth Trying#

Only for someone who already has clients or an audience — it's a monetization layer on existing demand, not a start-from-zero business. The guarantee + prepay mechanic is the fastest legitimate cash generator in the video.

Key actions#

  • Map your offer on the 6-type grid; add one adjacent category this month.
  • Draft a 5-slot, 12-month, guaranteed-outcome premium tier priced at full prepay with no discount.
  • Cap slots at ~20% of realistic demand — write the cap down before selling.
  • Convert cohort outcomes into testimonials, then fold the learnings into a digital product.
  • Plan the next launch to a larger slot count off the same base (5 → 10) instead of re-building demand.

Red flags#

  • "Work free until done" guarantee is an open-ended fulfillment liability — capped slots are the only thing making it survivable.
  • Requires existing demand: worthless to a brand-new account with no client base.
  • No verifiable receipts: unnamed portfolio companies, self-reported $85M.
  • Manufactured scarcity (selling 5 of 20) slides easily into fake-urgency marketing.

Novelty: NEW mechanic — "pent-up demand budgeting": deliberately selling only ~20% of known demand per launch to preserve future goodwill and grow cohort slot count. Controlled-liquidation discipline not previously covered. Secondary: prepaid guarantee as a cash-flow instrument (financing via customer prepayment).

4) The Only 4 Ways To Scale A Service Business (PICK ONE)#

Channel: Alex Hormozi | 611,118 views | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=O1As2zxy0es

Business model#

A scaling-vehicle decision framework for existing service businesses, not a startup idea. The 4 vehicles: (1) privately held chain (own every location), (2) franchise (sell name + systems + fee to investor-operators), (3) licensing of your playbook/IP (2 of the 3 franchise components — e.g., CrossFit = name+fee, no systems; white-label = systems+fee, no name), (4) software / tech-enabled service. Core thesis: pick ONE based on buildout cost per location, protectability of IP, cash-flow needs, and risk appetite.

Revenue model#

Given, extensively. Private chain: viable when buildout < ~$40–50K or < 6 months of first-year location profit (Insomnia Cookies: ~800 sq ft, huge flour/sugar margins). Franchise: ~$750K setup (legal/filings), ~$50K franchisee fee barely covers franchisee acquisition cost, money comes from royalties, needs 100+ locations — and 90–95% of franchises never pass 100. Licensing: near-zero cost, high margin, cash day one; exit multiple ~6x+ only with 80%+ yearly licensee retention. Software: highest exit multiple, same 80%+ retention bar. Case study: his portfolio company moved licensing → owned locations, $500K/yr → $12M/yr in 14 months, 12 locations.

Replication steps#

1. Confirm a profitable single-location service model with productizable (low-variability, low-skill) delivery.

2. Score the opportunity: buildout cost per location vs first-year profit; how copyable the offer; how certain the win.

3. Low buildout + near-certain win → open location #2 yourself and compound a private chain.

4. High buildout → franchise: ~$750K legal/setup, ~$50K franchisee fees, live on royalties; don't expect real profit until 100+ locations.

5. No capital + copyable offer → license the playbook (fee + name OR fee + systems, never all three or you're an illegal franchise); sign sticky contracts (80%+ yearly retention) to preserve exit value.

6. Only choose software with a technical co-founder — never outsource the build (his self-described "$3M mistake").

7. Re-evaluate annually: convert licensing to owned locations once licensees' economics prove out.

Automation potential: 4/10#

Org-design and capital vehicles, not automation plays. Chains/franchises add labor and ops drag per unit. Licensing is the most productizable (playbook + community ≈ semi-automatable); software demands an engineering org.

Quality: 8/10#

Receipts test: PASS (best in batch). Names verifiable real businesses (Insomnia Cookies, CrossFit, McDonald's, Gym Launch); his own portfolio is publicly consistent. The $500K→$12M flagship case is self-reported and anonymized, which keeps it off 9–10. No hype framing; he actively argues against the popular path.

Verdict: Mixed#

Not a business you can start this week — a decision lens that pays off only once you have a working service business. Highest-quality framework of the batch; the licensing→chain conversion pattern is the actionable nugget.

Key actions#

  • Run the buildout test: would location #2 cost <6 months of its projected first-year profit? If yes, own, don't license/franchise.
  • Check any "license my system" ambition against the 2-of-3 rule (name/systems/fee) to avoid illegal-franchise exposure.
  • Attach 80%+ yearly retention (auto-renew) to any licensing or software vehicle before expecting a real exit multiple.
  • Before paying an agency to build software, find a technical co-founder.
  • Watch licensees' unit economics: if they print money with low buildout, convert licenses to owned locations.

Red flags#

  • All dollar figures self-reported; flagship case-study company unnamed.
  • Franchise "lower failure rate" is common sales talk he himself debunks — but the content is still a funnel into Acquisition.com.
  • Framework is biased toward capital-rich operators; "just open more locations" assumes money and a proven single site.

Novelty: NEW theme — franchising as a scaling vehicle + IP/playbook licensing to operators (distinct from courses/creator monetization: you license a business system to business operators for fees + retention-based exit value). Neither is on the known list; graduated to candidate_themes.json for a dedicated future run. The 6-months-profit buildout heuristic is a useful rider.
