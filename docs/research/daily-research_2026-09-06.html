1. Boring Business Stacking (laundromat → vending → storage) - Harry's Stealth Wealth↑ top

- Business model: 45-min compilation (3 stacked videos) telling a recurring character's ("Harry") arc: buy an unglamorous local service business with seller financing, optimize operations, stack additional cash-flow assets until side income exceeds salary. Covers 18 types: laundromats, vending, self-storage, car washes, pest control, property management, ATMs, POD, courses, Airbnb arbitrage.

- Revenue model: narrative numbers, illustrative only: laundromat $198K purchase, $27K down + owner financing, $1,317/mo month 1 → $2,189/mo by month 6; 4 vending machines $1,096 → $2,413/mo; storage partnership (31% of build cost) $1,594/mo; stack crosses $6K/mo. Other ranges: ATMs $2.50-3.50/withdrawal (~$300-1,500/mo each), POD 70-90% margins, courses $1K-7K/mo, arbitrage $1K-3K/unit/mo, pressure washing $300-500/driveway, bookkeeping $300-600/client/mo, notary signings $75-200/each.

- Replication steps: (1) apply three filters - monthly repeat payment? low-drama purchase? own/control the asset?; (2) browse BizBuySell/local listings weekly, log asking price vs net income; (3) start at your capital level (ATM ~$3K, one vending machine ~$1.5-6K) rather than a $200K laundromat; (4) structure acquisitions with seller financing; (5) optimize (card readers, memberships, relocate underperformers); (6) reinvest and stack.

- Automation potential: 7/10 - the thesis is systems-over-hustle (ATMs, storage, card-system laundromats run near passively); service hustles stay manual.

- Quality: 3/10 - no receipts anywhere; "Harry" is an inconsistent fictional composite (ops coordinator → security guard → Memphis dispatcher across parts) presenting invented-but-realistic numbers as lived experience; headline stats (90% success, "98% for automated businesses," vending "95% 5-year survival") are unsourced and contradict known small-business failure data.

- Verdict: Mixed

- Key actions: use the three-filter test on any idea; log 5 real local listings with net income this week; an ATM or one vending machine is the cheapest test of the recurring-revenue thesis; ask retiring owners to finance.

- Red flags: fabricated-feeling narrative as true case study; unsourced statistics; $200-600K entries marketed to a $500-audience; vending/ATM metro saturation; padded compilation.
