## 1) Master this unsexy but PROFITABLE Claude use case

**Channel:** Blazing Zebra | 3,786 views | [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_o1QI6ITFPk](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_o1QI6ITFPk)

### Business model
Productized AI back-office compliance/filing service for small businesses. The use case: sell work SMBs *already pay outside firms for* where the output is objectively checkable — filed claims, reviewed leases, reconciled books, multi-state nonprofit registrations, trucking fuel-tax returns, sales tax, new-hire compliance, medical/dental admin. Deliver ~95% automated with Claude (built-in browser + Claude Code), replace sales calls with an AI-generated intake form (scope dropdowns) and a client-facing status dashboard. Pipeline: niche finder → define sellable unit → first jobs manual with human-in-the-loop → convert every caught error into a written rulebook rule → automate intake/status. Monetizes viewers via Patreon rulebook templates and a free-training email funnel.

### Revenue model
None given for the speaker's own business. Illustrative only: a 15-state nonprofit pays ~$5,000 for these services today; pricing at 30–50% of incumbent-firm fees implies ~$1,500–2,500 per filing. These figures are Claude-generated estimates, not earnings.

### Replication steps
1. Enable Claude's built-in browser; shortlist niches where (a) a business already pays an outside firm and (b) the deliverable has a verifiable done-state (a filed claim, a reviewed lease). No clear "done" = not a fit.
2. Prompt Claude to convert the niche into a sellable unit: intake fields, definition of done, failure modes, current market pricing; target per-unit pricing at 30–50% of the incumbent firm's fee.
3. Do the first jobs manually; once you hold both input and correct output, have Claude diff its draft against the verified version and log every miss into a miss database.
4. Write every caught error into a rulebook (done-state definition, input requirements, human-review flags, end-of-job review note, change log) — after a few dozen jobs this becomes the moat.
5. Use a meta-prompt to have Claude Code generate a styled intake form and client dashboard with dummy data; eliminate sales calls and "where's my job?" emails.
6. De-risk with liability-capping contract terms and cheap business insurance; optionally recruit a domain SME as paid consultant or revenue-share partner.

### Automation score: 9/10
The model is explicitly designed around ~95% automation with shrinking human review; only compliance judgment flags and final sign-off stay manual. (The first N jobs are deliberately manual — that's how the rulebook gets built.)

### Quality score: 3/10
Fails the receipts test: no named client business, no live dashboard of his own, no dollar earned — the $5K nonprofit pricing is a Claude-generated estimate. Sourcing is credible (credits Greg Eisenberg for the model; the Andon Labs case study is real and checkable), but the business itself is aspirational.

### Verdict: Mixed
Zero proof of revenue, but the selection filter (verifiable done-state + existing outsourced spend), the rulebook-as-moat mechanic, and per-unit discount pricing are concrete, cheap to test, and genuinely better thinking than generic "AI agency" content. Highest automation potential in today's batch by a wide margin.

### Key actions
- Steal the niche filter, not the video: list services where SMBs already pay outside firms AND the deliverable has a binary done-state (his top picks: nonprofit multi-state registration, trucking fuel-tax returns).
- Run one job manually with input → draft → diff-against-correct-output; every diff becomes a rulebook line. The rulebook, not the prompts, is the asset.
- Price per filing at 30–50% of the incumbent firm's quote; never hourly.
- Build the intake form + client dashboard early so sales and status support cost zero human time.
- Line up liability protection (contracts + insurance) before touching anything that is a legal filing.

### Red flags
- Zero revenue receipts — the entire business case rests on a Claude-generated pricing estimate.
- Real regulatory/liability exposure (tax filings, leases, compliance records); the cost of getting it wrong lands on you.
- The video itself is a monetization funnel (Patreon templates, email capture, SME-partner contact form) — the presenter's actual business is teaching this, not running it.
- "They won't just do it themselves with Claude" is asserted, not evidenced — the moat claim deserves skepticism as models improve.

### New mechanics vs. known themes
The core play is NOT new — AI back-office services sit inside covered themes. Three sub-mechanics are genuinely new:
1. **Verifiable-done-state niche filter** — only take work where the output is objectively checkable and a firm is already paid for it; explicitly excludes advice/subjective deliverables.
2. **Rulebook / miss-database as compounding moat** — every caught error becomes a written rule with review flags and change log, making the service harder to replicate with each job.
3. **Per-unit pricing as % of the incumbent firm's fee** — price the filing against what the outsourced firm charges, not time or tokens.

## 2) I Helped 100 People Get 1,000 Newsletter Subscribers (Without Ads)

**Channel:** Matt McGarry - GrowLetter | 11,831 views | [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aAmm5Bm_odI](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aAmm5Bm_odI)

### Business model
Newsletter-growth agency/consulting content marketing, delivered as a Q&A. Core asset is the "1K Challenge": a 30-day daily task list to reach 1,000 organic subscribers on one platform (LinkedIn or Instagram). Tactics: repurpose each weekly newsletter into 3+ posts; 5–10 comments/day on adjacent accounts; DM every new follower a newsletter link; 20 targeted LinkedIn connection requests/day; weekly pre-CTA teaser; weekly comment-to-get giveaway using an **email-gated back-catalog newsletter** as the lead magnet (VA DMs the link); weekly issue auto-published as a blog. At 5–10K subs: cross-promotions with matched unique clicks (±10%), re-run until parity, 2–3 recurring partners. North-star metric = revenue/verified clicks, not opens.

### Revenue model
None given for GrowLetter itself. Claims offered instead: "10M+ subscribers and $100M+ sales" added for clients (agency marketing claim); The Hustle's Trends product did "$15M+ ARR" when he worked there (historical, partially corroborated publicly); 30–50% of new followers convert via the DM script; benchmarks of 50% open / 5% CTR. Includes a Beehiiv affiliate link — affiliate income implied, no numbers.

### Replication steps
1. Pick ONE organic platform (LinkedIn or Instagram); niche two levels below your category (media → newsletters → newsletter growth) and stay there 6–12 months.
2. Daily (Mon–Fri): 1 repurposed post, 5–10 comments on adjacent accounts, DM every new follower with the newsletter-link script, 20 targeted LinkedIn connection requests.
3. Weekly: 1 pre-CTA teaser post 24h before send; 1 comment-to-get giveaway post whose lead magnet is an email-gated old newsletter; 1 issue (auto-published as a blog).
4. Budget 10–15 hrs/week for the first 30 days; a VA handles giveaway DMs; dial back content volume after day 30.
5. At 5–10K subs, list 10+ adjacent-but-non-competing newsletters and pitch cross-promos with click-matching terms: report unique clicks after 5 days; the short side re-promotes until clicks match within ~10%; refuse partners who won't agree.
6. Track revenue and bot-filtered verified clicks as the North Star; keep opens >50% / CTR >5% as hygiene thresholds; run a 3-question poll in every issue.

### Automation score: 5/10
DMing new followers, connection requests, comment seeding, and newsletter→post repurposing are scriptable or VA-delegable (he explicitly uses a VA for giveaway DMs); LinkedIn rate limits and relationship-building cap how far automation goes. The growth engine is deliberately manual.

### Quality score: 4/10
Named businesses and historical numbers (The Hustle/Trends) add some credibility, but GrowLetter's own results ("helped 100 people," "10M subscribers") are marketing claims with zero dashboards or named client examples in-video. Tactical stats (30–50% conversion, 2,000-comment posts) unverifiable.

### Verdict: Mixed
The 30-day task list and the click-matched cross-promo protocol are genuinely actionable, but everything rests on an unverifiable agency narrative, a survivorship-flavored "1K in 30 days" promise (he admits most people don't do it), and a 10–15 hr/week manual grind.

### Key actions
- Run the 30-day 1K Challenge on exactly one platform; copy the task list literally.
- Email-gate your best back-catalog issues and use them as comment-to-get lead magnets — zero new content cost.
- Structure every cross-promo as matched unique clicks (±10%), never post-for-post.
- Replace open-rate obsession with revenue + verified clicks; add a reply-bait poll each issue.

### Red flags
- Agency selling growth services — incentive to make growth look formulaic and fast.
- 1,000 subs in 30 days is presented as achievable while admitting "most people don't do it."
- Affiliate link (Beehiiv) embedded in the platform recommendation.
- No P&L shown for any method; anti-subscription/anti-ads takes serve his consulting positioning.

## 3) 6 Step Checklist To Build A $100,000+ Paid Newsletter in 2026

**Channel:** Nicolas Cole | 48,980 views | [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EAlOdXmIqcs](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EAlOdXmIqcs)

### Business model
Paid Substack newsletter layered on a free social "traffic engine." Cole runs two: Category Pirates and Write With AI. The six steps: (1) attach the paid topic to a free daily writing habit on X/LinkedIn; (2) make every issue *tangible* — sell an object (prompt pack, heat map, checklist, purchase list), not writing; (3) one-free-one-paid weekly cadence, harvesting Substack's network effects ("attention arbitrage" on VC money — Substack has raised $90M+); (4) send paid posts to the whole free list using paywall preview; (5) place the paywall at the cliffhanger, right before the promised asset is delivered; (6) repost one short-form piece daily to Substack Notes.

### Revenue model
Self-reported: Category Pirates ~$200K/yr ARR, Write With AI ~$300K/yr ARR; implied price point ~$20/mo; ~20% of Write With AI paid subscribers acquired from inside the Substack network. No third-party verification.

### Replication steps
1. Pick one topic and one traffic engine; write about the exact paid topic free on X/LinkedIn daily, CTA to the Substack, for 1–2 years.
2. Define the tangible asset per issue *before* writing: a prompt pack, keyword heat map, annotation guide, itemized purchase list — something "collectible" the reader wants new versions of forever.
3. Charge from day one (~$20/mo); publish 1 free + 1 paid issue weekly; pin your best-ever free piece to the top of the Substack.
4. Send each paid post to the entire list with a paywall preview at 20–50% of the post, cut exactly at the cliffhanger — free education in the preview, asset behind the paywall.
5. Repost one tweet/short post per day to Substack Notes; use Notes' revenue-attribution dashboard to see which posts convert free→paid.
6. Run the loop 18+ months; churn problems only appear if the asset isn't genuinely collectible.

### Automation score: 4/10
The moat is the author's daily human voice and judgment (asset design, editing, subject lines). Repurposing X→Notes, list segmentation, paywall mechanics, and analytics are automatable; the content core is not.

### Quality score: 5/10
Names real businesses (Category Pirates, Write With AI) and gives dollar figures — but they're self-reported ARR with no dashboards, MRR screenshots, or auditable receipts. The Substack-network 20% stat is his own analytics. Framework is internally coherent; numbers unverifiable.

### Verdict: Mixed
Sensible, proven-by-the-author playbook, but it's a 2-year daily-grind audience business with self-reported numbers — and newsletters are already a covered theme.

### Key actions
- Design the tangible, infinitely-collectible asset first; if you can't name it, don't launch.
- Cut the paywall at the cliffhanger, not at an arbitrary word count.
- Charge immediately; validate willingness-to-pay via the paid issue, not free content.
- Double-dip every short-form post to Substack Notes.

### Red flags
- All revenue figures self-reported; "probably 90% of why Write With AI grew" is unfalsifiable.
- Strategy presumes an existing social audience and 2 years of daily posting; cold-start odds not addressed.
- Substack "attention arbitrage" is a platform-dependency bet, not a moat.
- Heavy on consistency sermons, light on unit economics (no churn, LTV, or list-size math).

### Key actions

## 5) Zero to $10,000: Journey as an Indie Hacker [Micro SaaS Stories]

**Channel:** SaaS University | 8,902 views | [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=D7i2qnXAdmI](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=D7i2qnXAdmI)

### Business model
One-person productized design studio for SaaS startups ("Jim Designs" by Jim, a product designer with 10+ years experience). Unlimited design requests/revisions on a flat monthly subscription — the design-agency equivalent of DesignJoy.

### Revenue model
$10,000 *total* revenue claimed (explicitly not MRR). Current site pricing: $745/mo or $995/mo for unlimited requests/revisions, pause/cancel anytime. No dashboard, no MRR figure, no timeline.

### Replication steps
1. Pick a skill you already have 5–10+ years of paid experience in and an audience you personally understand (Jim: SaaS product design).
2. Skip the product build entirely — ship only a landing page; the "product" is client work delivered manually.
3. Price as a no-brainer vs. the alternative: undercut a full-time designer ($50–70K/yr salary) with a flat monthly subscription; initial goal is traction and learning, not margin.
4. Deliver visibly high-quality work (Dribbble-style portfolio) so referrals compound.
5. Later: add quarterly/annual pricing tiers to lock in revenue up front and raise prices as reputation builds.

### Automation score: 3/10
The deliverable is bespoke human design work; only intake, briefs, and revisions-tracking could be automated.

### Quality score: 4/10
Named business with a live site and visible pricing (verifiable), but the $10K figure is a self-reported story with no dashboard, timeline, or client evidence; the channel is also running a lead-magnet funnel ("sneaky micro SaaS ideas" download).

### Verdict: Mixed
Sound, proven playbook but nothing new (productized consulting / design subscription), thin receipts, and the video is partly a funnel for the channel's own lead magnet.

### Key actions
- If replicating: flat-rate unlimited-request subscription in a craft you're already senior at; landing page only, no product build.
- Price against the salary of the full-time hire you replace, not against other agencies.
- Add annual/quarterly prepay options early — the obvious missed lever.

### Red flags
- $10K total revenue is a milestone, not a business; no MRR, churn, or client count disclosed.
- $745/mo unlimited vs. 10 yrs seniority looks underpriced — margin risk at scale.
- Channel monetizes the story (lead magnet) — incentive to inflate the narrative.
