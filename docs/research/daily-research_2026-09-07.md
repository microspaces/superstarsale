## Strategy Analysis

### 1. Vintage Guitar Flipping Playbook - Jeremy Sheppard
- **Business model:** buy used/vintage guitars cheap from private sellers, resell at a markup through his shop (Bayou Music Exchange). Claims ~1,000 guitars flipped over 17-18 years.
- **Revenue model:** per-unit margin on flips. Only concrete number: negotiated a Gibson 335 at ~75% of ask (~$2,400 cash on a ~$3,200 ask implied) to preserve spread. No per-flip profit figures stated.
- **Replication steps:** (1) immerse in the niche community ("be a dork"); (2) apply the **30-year rule** - gear idolized 30 years ago hits peak demand when those fans hit peak income (he stockpiled 24-fret superstrats before the wave); (3) specialize in one segment (he sticks to Martin acoustics) to instantly spot mispriced units; (4) negotiate with physical cash (reliably extracts 10-25% discounts).
- **Automation potential:** 1/10 - relationship-driven, in-person sourcing, expert judgment.
- **Quality:** 6/10 - 17-18 years hands-on, a specific coherent heuristic, but zero receipts (no sold listings, no profit figures) and it's a 3-minute short.
- **Verdict:** Worth Trying - as a niche side hustle for someone who already knows the category.
- **Key actions:** pick one collectible niche you genuinely know; track the nostalgia cycle (aspirational ~30 years ago for people now 45-60); buy ahead of the curve, sell into peak; use cash offers to win discounts.
- **Red flags:** no profit disclosures; cash-only framing ignores tax/bookkeeping reality; returns depend on years of expertise and trend timing.

### 2. FBA Private Label vs Wholesale Decision Framework - Adam Pearson
- **Business model:** Amazon FBA seller comparing wholesale (reselling others' brands) vs private label (own-brand from China). Claims ~£250K/month revenue, £3.3-3.5M/year, 200+ products, 5 staff.
- **Revenue model:** private label margins 15-50% (avg 30-35%); wholesale 12-15% dropping to ~5% in price wars. His trajectory: wholesale ~£1M/yr at low margin → private label tripled revenue in 3.5 years, business now sellable for ~£1M cash (vs ~£0 enterprise value in wholesale). Client anecdote: £1K/month wholesale → +£2K/month after switching.
- **Replication steps:** (1) pick by goal - wholesale for quick side cash, private label for a sellable asset; (2) from zero, spend 3-6 months building ~£5K capital from a day job + side hustles; (3) deploy £1-2K into simple low-compliance inventory, rest into design/samples/launch; (4) order samples, don't cheap out on suppliers/packaging; (5) already in wholesale? Maintain it, don't grow it - put new energy into ONE own-brand SKU, then expand that winner across TikTok Shop/eBay/Shopify/US-EU.
- **Automation potential:** 4/10 - sourcing/PPC/replenishment semi-automatable; product selection and supplier relations stay manual.
- **Quality:** 6/10 - specific numbers throughout and a coherent both-models framework, but all self-reported with no screenshots, and the video funnels to a free 1-on-1 coaching call.
- **Verdict:** Mixed - genuinely useful decision framework, unverifiable numbers, he sells the exact transition he recommends.
- **Key actions:** decide goal first (income vs asset); one compliance-light product on ~£5K; wholesale veterans: maintain, don't grow, launch one own-brand SKU.
- **Red flags:** coaching-call funnel; self-reported revenue/exit claims; "match betting" side-hustle suggestion is grey-area and bannable; UK compliance glossed over.

### 4. Weekend Reselling Haul (Poshmark/eBay) - ShopNTales
- **Business model:** personal reselling vlog - full-time thrift flipping of women's clothing/shoes. This episode: 11 weekend sales ($523 gross; 5 eBay, 6 Poshmark). A haul, not a strategy lesson.
- **Revenue model:** item-level margins with receipts: $5 Chico's → $30; $2.49 bra → $25; $5 NWT Motel jeans → $67.50; $5 → $89 full ask; $5.99 leather shoes ($348 retail, missing strap) → $72. Typical 5-14x multiples on $5-6 buys; Poshmark takes 20%.
- **Replication steps:** (1) source at $2.49-5.99 price points, comp-check brands before buying; (2) learn category quirks (Chico's vanity sizing, mature brands like Misook); (3) photograph accurately with full measurements (her missing-strap shoes sat for months); (4) use offers/counters (10% to watchers, bundle counters); (5) small freebies for reviews.
- **Automation potential:** 2/10 - sourcing through shipping is hands-on; only cross-listing/offer-sending are semi-automatable (Vendoo/List Perfectly).
- **Quality:** 7/10 - refreshingly honest: real costs, real prices, admits bad buys and the 20% cut. More receipts than most guru content, though it's ~$523 gross/weekend, not a living.
- **Verdict:** Mixed - real unit economics worth studying, no systematized playbook.
- **Key actions:** comp-check at the rack; buy the $5-and-under tier of proven mid-tier brands; nail measurements to avoid dead inventory; targeted offers to convert stale listings.
- **Red flags:** weekend haul ≠ full-time income despite the framing; low $/hour unless scaled; 20% fee + returns risk; high saturation.

### 5. SMB Marketing, Cybersecurity & AI Toolkit (Panel) - Texas Economic Development
- **Business model:** not a pitch - a government/economic-development small-business panel (Workforce Solutions Texoma CTO, Sparklight ad consultant, May Media agency owner). Free education + free state/federal resources; agency panelists implicitly market themselves.
- **Revenue model:** none directly. Operational specifics: EDR auto-isolates hacked machines; cloud-backup restore drill takes ~1.5-2 days; $1M cyber insurance; a law-firm client runs an AI website chat agent that qualifies leads and books calls 24/7; **CISA offers free cybersecurity assessments and a free penetration test**; monthly simulated-phishing training.
- **Replication steps:** (1) security baseline: HTTPS everywhere, EDR on endpoints, cloud backups PLUS test restores via tabletop exercises, cyber insurance, monthly phishing simulations with a one-click report button; (2) marketing baseline: search-engine marketing for purchase intent, detailed targeting (realtor → banking/moving-company audiences), lead-form ad campaigns over boosted posts ("boosting is a postcard, ads capture leads"); (3) AI adoption: find the bottleneck first, then pick the tool (ChatGPT, Canva Magic Studio, Zapier, ClickUp); never paste raw AI output; **never feed real customer PII into AI tools** - use imaginary client profiles; (4) use CISA's free assessments and pen tests.
- **Automation potential:** 7/10 - the panel's core message is that AI is now a quiet everyday SMB advantage (lead-qualifying chat agents, Zapier workflows), after human-in-the-loop and data-governance guardrails.
- **Quality:** 8/10 - practitioners with named tools and real operational detail (restore-time drills, EDR behavior, a working AI-agent case study); no hype; explicitly warns against PII leakage and AI-written phishing. Weakness: almost no ROI figures; panelists have implicit sales interest.
- **Verdict:** Worth Trying - the most immediately actionable video of the batch for operating any small business, though it's civic education, not an opportunity.
- **Key actions:** book a free CISA assessment/pen test this month; test-restore your backups and measure actual recovery time; map your #1 workflow bottleneck and adopt one named tool against it with an edit-before-publish rule; replace post-boosting with lead-form campaigns; add an AI chat agent that qualifies and books.
- **Red flags:** panelists sell marketing services (implicit conflict); no ROI numbers; phishing training can't stop AI spear-phishing; PII-in-AI risk is real for regulated data (the workforce board itself banned AI note-takers entirely).
