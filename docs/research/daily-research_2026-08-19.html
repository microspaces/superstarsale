Video 1: Niche Pursuits - Boring Utility Websites ($35K+/Month)

Business model: Programmatic-SEO utility websites that solve one mundane task (unit conversions, typing tests, PDF tools), monetized with display ads or freemium subscriptions. Case studies: unitconverters.net (~$35K/mo, 4.5M visits/mo, display ads at ~$5 RPM), chartsheets.com (~$40K/mo selling $10-$170 spreadsheet templates), typingtest.com (~$44K/mo, 3.6M visits/mo, 8.05 pages/visit), smallpdf.com (~$12.5-17.5M/yr, 57M visits/mo, $12/mo freemium).

Revenue model:

- Display ads (RPM $5-$15 on tool/utility traffic): traffic x pageviews x RPM.

- One-time digital product sales (templates: $10-$170).

- Freemium SaaS subscription ($12/mo, tiny fraction of free users convert).

Steps to replicate:

1. Pick a boring, high-volume utility niche (converters, calculators, testers, generators).

2. Build hundreds of programmatic pages - one per query ("inches to feet", "kg to lbs").

3. Rank via Google organic search (80%+ of traffic for winners).

4. Monetize with display ads (Ezoic/Mediavine) or freemium upgrade.

5. Scale page count and query coverage; revenue compounds with indexation.

Automation potential: 9/10 - The most automatable model in today's batch. Page generation, internal linking, schema markup, and content assembly are all template-driven and scriptable with AI. Zero fulfillment, zero inventory, zero customer service. Main non-automatable input: SEO time-to-rank (3-12 months) and surviving Google core updates.

Quality score: 8/10 - Credible practitioner (Niche Pursuits is a well-known SEO brand) with SimilarWeb traffic data, named sites, and realistic revenue math (conservative $5 RPM, shown work). Not a course funnel. Caveat: estimates are third-party, and post-2024 Google updates make pure programmatic SEO riskier than when these sites peaked.

Verdict: ✅ Worth Trying

Key action items:

- Build a programmatic page generator: keyword matrix x page template x units/data = thousands of pages from one codebase.

- Start with a calculator/converter niche where each tool page earns repeat visits (like typingtest's 8 pages/visit) rather than one-and-done conversions.

- Validate with Google Keyword Planner + Ahrefs before building; target 10K+ monthly searches per cluster.

- Use AI to generate unique intro/FAQ content per page to reduce thin-content risk.

- Monetize early with Ezoic (no traffic minimum); switch to Mediavine at 50K sessions.

Video 2: Iman Gadzhi - "Growth Marketing" for Micro-Creators

You build their digital product (course/ebook/community) using AI tools, then take a 20-30% revenue share. Positioned as the ultimate beginner model: no audience, no face, no capital required - "AI does the heavy lifting."

Steps to replicate:

1. Find: build a list of micro-creators in monetizable niches (fitness, finance, hobbies).

2. Generate a "monetization game plan" per creator (audience audit + product proposal + revenue projection + 14-day launch calendar) - the value-first outreach asset.

3. Send the plan cold; sign creator on rev-share (Whop handles payments/splits).

4. Build: create the digital product with AI (offer structure, modules, worksheets, copy).

5. Launch: 14-day story-sequence (warm-up → value → open cart); creator promotes, you collect the split.

6. Systematize: monthly re-launches, new creators in parallel.

Automation potential: 7/10 - Product build, game-plan generation, and launch copy are all AI-automatable end-to-end (the video itself demos an AI agent doing the audit). The persistent human-in-loop steps: creator outreach, negotiating splits, and maintaining the relationship. Deal flow is the bottleneck, not production.

Quality score: 5/10 - The model is real (Whop revenue-share deals exist and the creator-economy math is plausible), but the video is a polished funnel for consulting.com ($25K enterprise clients) and his AI tools. Testimonials are cherry-picked; "results not typical" is stated outright. Extract the model; ignore the packaging.

Verdict: ⚠️ Mixed - model is sound and semi-automatable, but income claims are inflated marketing.

Key action items:

- Build the AI "monetization game plan" generator yourself (scrape creator's content → audience analysis → product proposal → projected P&L → 14-day launch plan) - this is the entire moat and it's one good prompt chain.

1. Do the audience-price math: goal ÷ price = sales needed; sales ÷ conversion rate (1%) = audience needed.

4. Focus 20% of marketing on the high-ticket offer.

Key Takeaways

1. Programmatic utility websites are today's clear automation winner (9/10). One codebase generates thousands of tool pages; Google sends free traffic; display ads/freemium convert it. The entire pipeline - page generation, internal linking, content uniqueness - is scriptable with AI today. Main risks: SEO time-to-rank (3-12 months) and Google core-update volatility. Mitigate with genuinely useful tools (repeat-visit testers/calculators) rather than thin converter pages.

2. The "growth marketer" rev-share model is the highest-leverage hybrid (7/10). AI automates the product build, the outreach asset (monetization game plan), and launch copy end-to-end; only creator outreach and deal terms stay human. Build the game-plan generator yourself - it's one good prompt chain, not a $25K consulting program.

3. Physical "sleep income" (ATMs, vending, storage) is passive but NOT laptop-automatable. Treat it as a later capital-deployment tier. The transferable insight is the capital ladder: start with zero-capital digital assets, stack profits, buy cash-flowing physical systems later.

4. Universal math check before any launch (from Video 3): revenue goal ÷ price = sales needed; sales ÷ 1% conversion = audience needed. Run this before building anything - it kills bad ideas in 30 seconds and prices them correctly.

5. Cross-video pattern: every credible model reduces to "build once → platform fulfills → traffic compounds." The automatable spine is: AI content generation → programmatic or marketplace distribution → automated monetization (ads/checkout/splits). Build that spine once; every future product plugs into it.
