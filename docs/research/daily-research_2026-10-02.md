## 2) 3-Step Lead Magnet Tutorial: How To Create A High-Converting Lead Magnet

**Channel:** Alex Cattoni | 106,952 views | [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7orRtqwKIBw](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7orRtqwKIBw)

### Business model
Copywriting-education channel teaching a lead-magnet funnel: every lead magnet must (1) solve an immediate need, (2) create desire via "useful but incomplete" content, (3) sell. Four desire mechanisms: solve step one free (Frank Kern's Results in Advance), diagnose an unknown problem (quiz), give the what/sell the how (webinar/masterclass), free sample/discount. Funnel: free content → email list → paid challenge (her 5 Day Write & Ignite Challenge).

### Revenue model
None given — no dollar figures anywhere; examples are qualitative (Dr. Michael Breus chronotype quiz, Mindvalley webinar, Lunya $20-off popup, her own Hot Offer Checklist).

### Replication steps
1. Identify your prospect's most desired end result; break it into steps and pick step one to give away free.
2. Build the lead magnet in one of four formats: step-one guide/checklist, diagnostic quiz, masterclass/webinar, or discount/sample.
3. Put it behind a landing page (AIDA) and deliver instantly.
4. Embed a CTA inside the magnet plus an email follow-up sequence selling step two (the paid offer); segment with quiz data if using a quiz.

### Automation potential: 6/10
Once built, the magnet + landing page + email sequence runs evergreen with no marginal labor; content creation and traffic acquisition remain manual (quiz generation is now AI-scriptable, nudging this higher).

### Quality (receipts test): 6/10 — medium
Standard direct-response framework, but she cites real, verifiable named assets (her own checklist/challenge, Breus quiz, Mindvalley, Lunya); no dashboards or revenue numbers, but also no unverifiable dollar claims.

### Verdict: **Worth Trying**
The only immediately executable build in the batch, and the most automatable: a one-day build that runs evergreen.

### Key actions
- Ship one lead magnet that solves step one of your existing paid offer this week — don't build the product first.
- Prefer a diagnostic quiz: it converts AND captures segmentation data for follow-up emails.
- Put the sale CTA both inside the magnet and in the email sequence.
- Use "give the what, sell the how" to position your paid product as the implementation shortcut.

### Red flags
- Zero revenue data — the method is asserted, not measured.
- Traffic generation (the hard part) isn't covered at all; the $20-off popup example is a weak, commoditized pattern.

## 4) How Godwin Facility Services Uses the Sherpa Drone Featuring Ryan Godwin

**Channel:** Lucid Bots | 15,632 views | [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-kdp7ugUcEc](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-kdp7ugUcEc)

### Business model
Drone-assisted exterior cleaning (pressure washing / facility services). The Lucid Bots Sherpa cleaning drone replaces boom lifts and manual crews for large commercial facades; the drone doubles as a service-delivery tool and a marketing differentiator to win enterprise contracts.

### Revenue model
Self-reported by operator Ryan Godwin: $150–170K/yr (2-man team) pre-drone → ~$500K/yr now (~3 years, +20–30% in year one) → ~$2M in quoted/projected work slated next year; ~$250K of work booked for October alone; drone saves ~20% (±5%) vs lift rentals (~$5K/lift rental avoided); advised entry point: already at ~$200K/yr revenue. Named clients: Johnson & Johnson, Domino Sugar, Space Needle.

### Replication steps
1. Have (or start) an exterior cleaning business already doing ~$200K/yr so the drone purchase doesn't hurt working capital.
2. Buy the Sherpa outright (vendor-recommended path); do not buy and go straight into a paying job.
3. Train 1–2+ weeks; partner with local operators who already run a Lucid drone or hire a consultant — the drone-cleaning community is small and cooperative.
4. Use the drone as the sales wedge: demo it, market it, pitch bigger commercial accounts you couldn't reach with lifts; treat the first couple of jobs (with a hired helper) as tuition.
5. Scale by stacking drone capability into proposals — speed and coverage beat lift-based competitors on turnaround.

### Automation potential: 4/10
Hardware automates the hazardous lift work (~20% cost saving) but every job still needs a human pilot, and sales are manual relationship selling.

### Quality (receipts test): 7/10 — medium-high
Named operator, named business, named marquee clients (real receipts), but it's a vendor-produced case study (Lucid Bots sells the drone) and all revenue figures are self-reported with no dashboard shown.

### Verdict: **Mixed**
Real business with real clients, but capital-gated, vendor-narrated, and only marginally automatable.

### Key actions
- Only enter if you already run a ~$200K/yr cleaning operation and can buy the drone in cash.
- Budget 1–2+ weeks of training; first jobs with a hired helper are a loss leader.
- Pitch the drone as a differentiator into enterprise/landmark accounts (the actual margin unlock).
- Film the drone in action — the novelty itself generated year-one growth as free marketing.

### Red flags
- Entire video is a Lucid Bots marketing piece (they profit from drone sales, not from your success).
- All dollar figures self-reported, none verifiable; "buy one and go try it" exhortation from the vendor; unprofitable learning curve on first jobs; capital-gated entry.

## 5) The $400 Grease Trap Business Restaurants Are LEGALLY Forced To Hire

**Channel:** Harry's Stealth Wealth | 5,088 views | [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jBiYkNOxHyQ](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jBiYkNOxHyQ)

### Business model
Licensed commercial grease trap (FOG) pumping route. Restaurants are legally required to keep grease interceptors below 1/4 capacity and only licensed haulers may pump them — regulation, not relationships, drives demand. Revenue per stop is doubled: paid by the restaurant to haul waste, then paid again by renderers/biodiesel processors for the used cooking oil (UCO) collected.

### Revenue model
$400/stop; 30–40 stops/week per truck at route density; ~$350/quarterly pump = ~$1,400/yr per account (~$117/mo recurring) on 12-month auto-renewing contracts; 86 accounts ≈ $10K/mo, 256 accounts ≈ $30K/mo; inflection point ~20 committed accounts (below = "a vehicle," above = "a route"). UCO resale: bulk UCO spiked above $1,400/metric ton in early 2026 (SAF demand). Costs: used vacuum truck ~$60K; licensing stack $2K–7K over 6–16 weeks (USDOT $0, MC authority ~$300 + $25–50 agent, state EPD registration ~$200/yr, Class B CDL + tanker endorsement $1.5K–4K); market cited at $1.42B growing ~6%/yr.

### Replication steps
1. Buy a used vacuum truck (medium-duty chassis, 800–1,000 gal steel tank, mid-range pump) — ~$60K.
2. Stack the three licensing layers: USDOT (free) → motor carrier authority (~$300) → state transporter registration (pick a streamlined state: TX/FL/GA/TN/NC over NY/DE) → driver's Class B CDL with tanker endorsement ($1.5K–4K). Budget $2K–7K and 6–16 weeks.
3. Week 1: get 3 bids from rendering plants/FOG processors for a disposal contract; set up dispatch software (ServiceCore or Routific); open business checking.
4. Week 2: visit the local wastewater authority's FOG program coordinator to get on the approved-hauler list; walk 15–20 independent restaurants/day within 5–10 miles; pitch beating current rate by 10–15% with auto-filed compliance manifests on a quarterly recurring schedule.
5. Weeks 3–4: execute first services, issue manifests, collect referrals + Google reviews, cluster accounts, and drive to the ~20-account density threshold; sell collected UCO to renderers as the second revenue line.

### Automation potential: 3/10
Dispatch/route software and auto-filed manifests are light tooling; the core is physical truck work by a CDL driver, and acquisition is door-to-door walking.

### Quality (receipts test): 5/10 — medium
Internally consistent math and real cited sources (National Restaurant Association rule, ServiceCore "Toilet Talk" podcast operator, usedvacuumtrucks.com, Growth Market Reports), but the operator's numbers are second-hand, there is no live dashboard, and disposal pricing is admittedly unpublished — the dollar claims are unverifiable.

### Verdict: **Mixed**
The most complete replication playbook in the batch (costs, week-by-week plan, density threshold) — but zero first-party receipts, heavy capex, and a 6–16 week license wait before dollar one.

### Key actions
- Pick ONE zip code, drive it before lunch, count independent restaurants × $1,400/yr = the saturation revenue math before committing.
- Get on the sewer authority's approved-hauler list FIRST — the highest-leverage hour of the build.
- Negotiate disposal contracts via phone (3 bids, never published pricing) in week 1.
- Structure every account as quarterly auto-renew recurring and cluster geographically to hit 20+ accounts.

### Red flags
- Faceless "stealth wealth" channel monetizing the idea; the featured operator is second-hand (podcast) with no receipts shown.
- The $1,400/account math ignores disposal expense (unknown until negotiated); regulatory timeline varies sharply by state; UCO price is commodity-volatile (the SAF-driven spike can reverse); heavy capex + CDL before revenue.
