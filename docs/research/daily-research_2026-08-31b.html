1. AI-Assisted Passive Content Portfolio (KDP books, affiliate blog, evergreen YouTube) — Shane Hummus#

Business model: A portfolio of "stock-it-once" content assets — self-published Amazon KDP books (AI-ghostwritten with Claude), affiliate niche blogs, evergreen buying-decision YouTube videos, and "document your job" channels — each earning royalties/commissions/ad revenue from search traffic for years.

How money is made:

  • KDP royalties: 3 revenue lines per book (ebook download, paperback, audiobook). Case study: Rachel Richards — 2 books, ~25K copies each, $97K/year in royalties years after publication, launched with $75 in ads.
  • Affiliate niche blog: Michelle Schroeder Gardner (Making Sense of Cents) — ~$40K/month semi-passive; one old "stay-at-home jobs" post still pulls tens of thousands of monthly readers.
  • YouTube "toll booth" affiliate: his WGU degree-review series peaked at $10K/month and still pays thousands monthly 2–3 years later; $1M+ lifetime affiliate + $1.5M AdSense. Organic Chemistry Tutor (evergreen answer videos): ~$79K/month AdSense, posts once every 2–3 months.
  • Document-your-job channels: brother Zach (HVAC → trades-careers channel) $0 → $18K/month within months; Scotty Kilmer ~$24K/month AdSense ($24M lifetime).

Steps to replicate:

1. Pick a narrow, purchase-intent niche (his rule: "productivity for medical workers," not "productivity").

2. Use the Claude interview-prompt stack: paste prompt → "interview me until you have enough information" → outline → draft → human edit/fact-check pass → publish.

3. KDP: 1 niche book per quarter, let royalties compound; $50–100 test ads.

4. YouTube: find questions thousands search weekly that nobody has answered with "boring spreadsheet work" (his WGU credit-mapping example), record 5 → upload 5 → repeat; affiliate links in description.

5. Add social distribution (Pinterest, email, FB groups) on top of search traffic.

Automation potential: 7/10 — The production side (research, outlines, drafts, SEO structure) is now heavily AI-automatable; delivery (Amazon/Google/YouTube) is fully platform-automated. Remaining human work: fact-checking, voice/persona, and front-loaded volume.

Quality score: 8/10 — Named, verifiable case studies with real numbers (CNBC-covered KDP author, public blog income reports, his own screenshots). Deductions: it's also a funnel for his coaching, and "passive" requires years of compounding.

Verdict: Worth Trying — specifically the evergreen purchase-intent YouTube affiliate engine; the KDP and blog variants are slower and more saturated.

Key action items: Build a topic-research pipeline (search-volume + weak-competition purchase queries) → Claude interview-prompt drafting → human edit → batch-record 5 evergreen videos/fortnight → affiliate links + AdSense. Revisit the WGU-style "cross-reference spreadsheet nobody wants to do" angle as a differentiator.

Steps to replicate:

2. First channel: Facebook Marketplace (no fees, no shipping) → graduate to Etsy for reach (learn shipping).

3. Add a booth only after validating (vet antique malls; beware rent+commission eating margin); markets for pricing tests and mall connections; live selling last (needs audience).

Automation potential: 3/10 — Physical sourcing, photographing, listing, packing, and booth merchandising are hands-on. Listing/cross-posting and repricing can be tool-assisted (Vendoo-style), but the core is manual labor.

Quality score: 6/10 — Genuinely practical barrier-by-barrier breakdown (imposter syndrome, shipping fear, pricing, taxes, perfectionism) from an operating reseller; no revenue numbers in this video.

Verdict: Skip — fails the automation mandate; only relevant if flipping is already a running division (the cross-posting/repricing tooling angle).

Key action items (if pursued anyway): FB Marketplace first → Etsy second; comp-based pricing; automate only cross-listing and sold-item relists.

3. Price per workload unit (pages), not per seat — aligns cost with value, attracts B2B volume.

4. Validate with a few hundred dollars of Google Search ads on exact-problem keywords.

5. Keep improving parser coverage (more banks/formats) — each expansion widens the moat with near-zero marginal support.

Automation potential: 9/10 — The product is automation (sold to businesses automating their own workflow). Self-serve signup, usage-based billing, free-tier lead gen, ~zero-touch operations. Founder's remaining work: algorithm coverage + light marketing.

Quality score: 7/10 — Secondhand (Indie Hackers writeup narrated by the channel), but with concrete, checkable numbers (public product, pricing page, MRR chart, cost breakdown). Micro-SaaS is a known theme, but this is the cleanest evidence-backed unit economics of the set.

5. AI Onboarding Bot for Paid Discord Communities ("Salesy") — UnityXpert#

Business model: The video is a product demo/pitch for Salesy (SalesGenie): a Discord bot that auto-opens a private ticket/thread for every new member and runs a 1:1 AI onboarding conversation — welcome → qualify (goals/struggles) → answer doubts → pitch the paid membership personalized to their goal; handles objections, offers lead magnets by email capture, and schedules automated follow-ups for "I'll buy next month" members. Built-in CRM dashboard with lead scores, emails captured, next-follow-up dates.

How money is made (two layers):

  • For community owners: higher paid-membership conversion + retention (members understand value before churn-window). The problem stated: members join, get dumped into channels, never engage, leave.
  • For the vendor: SaaS subscription to the bot itself.

Steps to replicate:

1. For any paid community: replace channel-dump onboarding with an auto-ticket 1:1 DM flow (bot opens thread on join).

2. Script the funnel: 3 qualify questions → value answers → personalized upgrade pitch.

3. Objection branch → lead magnet (free resource, email capture) → CRM record.

4. "Future buyer" branch → store promised date → automated follow-up on that date, re-open ticket if no purchase.

5. Or build this as a sellable micro-SaaS for the paid-community niche (trading, coaching, courses servers).

Automation potential: 8/10 — The entire sales/onboarding flow is automated end-to-end (bot + LLM + CRM + scheduled follow-ups); buildable today with a Discord bot, an LLM API, and a scheduler.

Quality score: 4/10 — Pure vendor demo; zero customer numbers, no pricing shown, no independent evidence. The pattern is credible, the evidence is not.

Verdict: Mixed — as a product claim, unproven; as a conversion tactic to bolt onto an existing paid community (or as a niche micro-SaaS build), genuinely worth testing.

Key action items: If running any paid community: implement auto-ticket onboarding with 3 qualifying questions and a scheduled future-buyer follow-up this week (LLM + Discord API + cron). As a build: this is a viable "AI tools" micro-SaaS aimed at community operators.

The replicable formula is own-painful-workflow → one-input/one-output web app → per-workload-unit pricing ($30–99/mo) → no-signup free tier → exact-match search ads for validation.

2. Automated 1:1 onboarding is the highest-leverage conversion surface for paid communities — auto-ticket on join, 3 qualifying questions, objection branch with lead-magnet email capture, and date-stamped future-buyer follow-ups. Fully buildable with a Discord bot + LLM + scheduler; equally viable as a niche micro-SaaS for community operators.
